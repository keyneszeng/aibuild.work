# AI Product Manager Advanced Bootcamp — Course Overview

> For product managers and founders with product experience. The goal is not "knowing how to use AI tools," but "knowing how to design AI products."

## I. Course Positioning

This course is an advanced training program oriented toward **product design and delivery**, not "an intro to using AI tools." A huge amount of content on the market stops at "use ChatGPT to write your weekly report" or "use Cursor to write code." But the truly scarce capability is: turning AI's uncertainty, probabilistic nature, and emergent abilities into a product plan that engineering, algorithms, data, and operations can actually collaborate on and ship.

We borrow several mature open-source resources as the underlying knowledge base:

- **Product-Manager-Skills** (deanpeters): 77 battle-tested PM frameworks and the Triple Diamond methodology, providing structured deliverable patterns for PRD, user stories, opportunity trees, JTBD, prioritization, and more.
- **Awesome AI Product Management** (aza-ali): tools, mental models, and a reading list for AI PMs (*Inspired* / *Empowered* / *The Coming Wave* / *AI Product Playbook* / *Art of AI Product Development*) plus industry reports (Sequoia's "Services: The New Software," Stanford AI Index).
- **ai-agent-camp** (minicoohei): AI Agent literacy for non-engineering roles — LLM principles, Tokens, Agent, Context Engineering, MCP, multimodality, RAG, hallucination prevention, and safety.
- **easy-vibe** (datawhalechina): the path from idea to prototype validation (Double Diamond, Jobs to Be Done, The Mom Test).
- **Prompt-Engineering-Guide** (dair-ai): the spectrum of prompting techniques such as Zero-shot / Few-shot / CoT / ReAct / RAG / Function Calling.

This course **recombines the above knowledge into deliverables a PM can execute**: every lesson requires producing a product document, a flowchart, or a metrics table — not stopping at concepts.

## II. Target Audience

| Persona | Current state | Pain point | What this course gives them |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM with 2–5 years of experience | Knows requirements and PRD, but "can't understand or catch" AI projects | Can't tell whether an AI feature should use RAG or an Agent; can't evaluate model quality; struggles to talk with algorithms | AI product capability map + shippable PRD/RAG/Agent templates |
| Founder / business owner | Wants to use AI to transform the business, but put off by technical jargon | Doesn't know what AI can solve, how much it costs, or where the risks are | Opportunity-analysis method + commercialization plan template |
| Traditional software PM in transition | Has built deterministic systems, building a probabilistic system for the first time | Struggles to accept "uncertain results," doesn't know how to build fault tolerance or human review | Evaluation system + risk checklist + human-in-the-loop design |

## III. Core Pain Points (What We Solve)

1. **Can only use tools, can't design products**: treats "have AI write copy" as a product plan, lacking user scenarios, metrics, boundaries, and risk control.
2. **Treats AI like traditional software**: ignores uncertainty, hallucination, latency, and cost — it breaks the moment it launches.
3. **Missing deliverables**: no AI-version PRD, Agent flowchart, or evaluation test set, so engineering/algorithms can't take over.
4. **Blind spot in evaluation**: only "feels about right," with no quantifiable hit rate, human-intervention rate, or hallucination rate.
5. **Collaboration aphasia**: doesn't understand RAG, vector databases, Agent, or context engineering, making meetings with algorithms/engineering inefficient.

## V. Deliverables per Lesson (Red Lines)

- **Not just concepts**: each lesson must produce one of — **product document / flowchart / metrics table**.
- **Reflect AI characteristics**: must cover the four dimensions of **uncertainty, evaluation, fault tolerance, and human intervention** — not just showing off techniques.
- **Real user value**: every feature plan must answer "for whom, what real problem does it solve, how do we measure success."
- **Structured artifacts**: lessons 01–10 each contain 12 required sub-modules (Learning Goals, Warm-up Question, Core Concepts, Product Case Demo, Design Method, PRD Template Snippet, Practice Task, Prompt Template, Human Review Checklist, Common Mistakes, Homework, Grading Rubric).
