# Lesson 1 · AI Tools Primer → My 10 High-Frequency Work Scenarios

## I. Learning Goals

- **Knowledge**: Know what AI is, what it can do, and what it can't; distinguish "conversational AI" from "AI assistants built into office suites"; understand the concept of "hallucination."
- **Skill**: Use this system's standard Prompt structure to break a job task into "specific tasks AI can help with."
- **Output**: Produce the "My 10 High-Frequency Work Scenarios" list (with scenario, pain point, and the part AI can help with).

## II. Warm-up Question

> Think back over yesterday: which tasks were "repetitive, time-consuming, yet unavoidable"?
> For example: writing a group notice, organizing a chat log, turning customer questions into a table, writing three lines of progress for your boss…
>
> Spend 2 minutes listing the 5 things that pop into your head on paper. By the end of this lesson, you'll have a 10-item list, and for each you'll know how to get AI to lend a hand.

## III. Core Concepts

### 1. What is "Generative AI" (in one sentence)

You give it a text instruction (called a "prompt"), and it returns text / a table / a plan. The clearer you ask, the more reliable the answer.

### 2. Three types of AI you can use right away

- **Chat-box type** (e.g., ChatGPT, Wenxin Yiyan, Tongyi Qianwen, Kimi, Doubao, etc.): you type, it answers — best for writing, editing, summarizing, translating, and brainstorming.
- **Built into office tools**: WPS/Word's "AI rewrite," Excel/Feishu Sheets' "smart analysis," meeting-minutes software — no need to switch tools, use them in place.
- **Specialized type**: dedicated tools for images, video, and spreadsheets, which later lessons will use.

### 3. The most critical word: Hallucination

AI sometimes **confidently fabricates false information** — fake data, fake names, fake policies, fake citations. It isn't "bad"; it's that "it doesn't know what it doesn't know." So:

> **Any numbers, names, regulations, contract clauses, or medical advice must be verified by you personally.**

### 4. A general rule of thumb: break "big tasks" into "small instructions"

"Help me write a weekly report" is too vague. A better breakdown:

- First have AI list the report structure →
- You fill in what you did this week →
- Have AI polish it into a formal tone →
- You check the numbers and wording.

This is the everyday use of "human judgment, AI assistance."

## IV. Real Work Scenario Demo

### Case 1: The admin's "weekly notice hell"

**Scenario**: Every Monday, post a "this week's meeting schedule" notice to 3 groups — same format but slightly different content; handwritten, 20 minutes.
**AI approach**: Paste the meeting list to AI and ask it to "generate notices with different tones for Group A / Group B." Three versions in 1 minute; she only changes the times.
**Red-line reminder**: Group names and attendee names are internal information — when sharing the case externally, mask them as "a certain department's group."

### Case 2: The salesperson's "forgotten client follow-up"

**Scenario**: 40 WeChat clients in hand; who needs a follow-up and what each said last time all rely on memory.
**AI approach**: Organize the chat highlights into a table (client / last contact / next step / suggested time) and let AI prioritize.
**Red-line reminder**: Chat logs contain customer privacy — **do not use public AI to process real client names and phone numbers**; use "Client A / Client B" for practice.

### Case 3: The operator's "topic block"

**Scenario**: Must produce 3 articles a week; coming up with headlines takes a whole afternoon.
**AI approach**: Give AI your product's selling points and target audience, have it produce 20 headlines + angles at once, and you pick 3.

## V. Hands-on Task

- **Background**: You're newly hired / currently on the job, drowning in trivial tasks every day.
- **Input**: The 5 most exhausting things on your mind (listed in the warm-up question).
- **Steps**:
  1. Use the template below to have AI break each item into "subtasks AI can take over."
  2. Expand to 10 items, marking "AI handles / you handle" for each.
  3. Pick 3 high-frequency scenarios to "try this week."
- **Expected output**: A "My 10 High-Frequency Work Scenarios" table.

### This Lesson's Prompt Template (Standard Structure)

```
You are now playing the role of: an efficiency coach skilled at handing repetitive work to AI
My background is: I am a [your role], with lots of repetitive writing / communication / organizing work every day, and I don't know code
The task I need to complete is: break my listed work items one by one into "specific subtasks AI can help with" and "human judgment I must do myself"
The target user / reader is: myself (for daily efficiency)
Existing materials are as follows: [paste the 5 things you wrote in the warm-up question]
Please output: a table with columns: No. / work item / part AI can take over / part I must check myself / one-line suggested AI instruction
Output format: Markdown table
Constraints: no real names, client names, phone numbers, or other privacy; methods only, no code
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

## VI. Human Review Checklist

- [ ] The 10 items in the list genuinely come from my real work, not made up.
- [ ] Each item is marked "AI handles / I handle," and decision-making isn't fully handed to AI.
- [ ] No real client names, phone numbers, contracts, salaries, or other sensitive info appears.
- [ ] I picked the 3 "try this week" items and have concrete actions.
- [ ] I understand "hallucination": I know which outputs must be human-verified.

## VII. Common Mistakes

1. **Using AI as a search engine**: asking "help me look up the latest 2024 tax law" — AI may make it up. Instead: "based on common situations for general taxpayers, help me list a self-check checklist, and mark 'subject to the latest official tax authority documents.'"
2. **Instructions too vague**: "help me write a proposal" — output is hollow. Fill in background, reader, and format.
3. **Pasting secrets directly**: dropping an Excel with client lists into public AI. Red line!
4. **Copying without checking**: sending AI's data straight to the boss. Always double-check.
5. **Expecting one-shot perfection**: complex tasks need "break down + iterate"; don't demand a perfect answer at once.
