# Lesson 6 · Spreadsheets & Data → Data Summaries / Business Analysis

**For**: Admins, finance staff, store managers, sole proprietors, small-team leads (no formula-writing or coding needed)
**Recommended duration**: 90 min (40 min lecture + 50 min hands-on)

## I. Learning Goals

- **Knowledge**: Understand that "reading data" has three layers — **read the numbers (what happened) → attribute the cause (why) → recommend (so what)**. AI is best at expressing the first and third layers; the second layer must be your judgment. Master "explain what each column means before pasting a table to AI" (provide context). Understand how important it is that AI **can't do arithmetic** — it is a "language model," not a "calculator," so you must verify every number yourself.
- **Skill**: Turn a monthly report into a "data summary script" your boss can grasp in three minutes; design which metrics and how to read each for a "business-analysis dashboard" from scattered operational numbers; get AI to write the *how-to* steps for "which Excel formula / how to build a pivot table" instead of letting it calculate for you.
- **Output**: A *Data-Summary Script* (ready to send to a group or report) + a *Business-Analysis Dashboard Spec* (lists metrics, definitions, interpretation, and warning thresholds).

> This lesson references Prompt-Engineering-Guide's real frameworks — "Information Extraction," "Text Summarization," "Reasoning / Mathematics (the limits of reasoning and math)," and "Factuality (factual-risk)" — with one key reminder: AI gets arithmetic wrong, a well-known weakness, so this lesson makes "you verify the numbers" a hard red line.

## II. Warm-up Question

> Month-end: you've finished the spreadsheet, and your boss asks "so?" — can you only say "uh… it's all in the table"?
> You have sales records, a client list, and a cost sheet, but can't say "was this month good or bad, and where exactly did it fall short"?
> Or the opposite: you know perfectly well how the business is doing, but the moment you write the report it becomes a pile of numbers nobody can read?

Most people's problem isn't "no data" — it's that **nobody walks the road from data to conclusion for them**. In this lesson, AI does three things for you: **translate numbers into plain language, organize scattered metrics into a dashboard, and write the conclusion into report-ready words.** Arithmetic and attribution stay with you.

## III. Core Concepts

### 1. AI is not a calculator — the first red line of this lesson
Handing "what is 1234 × 0.87" to AI, it may give you a number that looks right but is wrong. **Do all sums, percentages, and period-over-period comparisons in Excel, or on your own calculator.** AI's value is: you give it the numbers, and it helps you *interpret*, *organize*, and *express*. Remember this division of labor and you won't have a major accident.

### 2. Before pasting a table, explain "what each column is"
Paste a pile of numbers straight to AI and it will guess blindly. The right move is to give a "data dictionary" first:
> "Below is our June store transaction flow. Column A = date, B = foot traffic, C = number of orders, D = sales amount (RMB), E = return amount (RMB)."
State the definitions and AI's interpretation accuracy jumps a tier immediately. That is "providing context."

### 3. The "three-part script" for a data summary
A reportable data summary is always these three parts:
- **Conclusion**: how the month went overall (one sentence, with the single most critical number)
- **Highlights & problems**: what's good (2 points), what's bad (2 points), each with a number
- **Next step**: so I suggest doing what (1–2 concrete actions)
Numbers with no "so what" is not a report — it's just handing over a table.

### 4. A business dashboard = choosing the right 5 metrics
A dashboard is not "more metrics are better" — it's "fewer is better, but you must pick right." A small business usually watches these five:
- **How much came in** (foot traffic / leads)
- **How much converted** (conversion rate)
- **How much per order** (average order value)
- **How much came back** (repurchase rate / returning customers)
- **How much is left** (gross / net margin)
Have AI help you fill these five boxes with concrete metrics + definitions + warning thresholds for your own business.

### 5. Let AI teach you the operation, not calculate for you
"How do I summarize sales by month in this sheet?" — have AI give you the *pivot-table click steps* or the *formula syntax*; you execute in Excel, so the result is Excel's calculation and is trustworthy. This is a hundred times safer than letting AI quote the number directly.

### 6. Treat anomalous numbers as "suspect," don't "invent causes"
AI seeing "July sales down 30%" will naturally hand you a pile of reasons: "could be seasonality, could be competitor promotions" — **all of this is guessed.** Only you know the real cause (the store was under renovation that week; that big client order was pushed to August). So the dashboard needs a column called "Attribution (filled by human)."

## IV. Real Work Scenario Demo

### Case 1: A store's monthly report from "a pile of numbers" to "three sentences"
**Source material** (a sole proprietor's notes): "June flow 186k, May was 212k. About 1400 came in, 620 orders. Returns over 8k. Weekends much better than weekdays."
**AI approach**: Paste the data + state definitions, ask for "a three-part data summary: conclusion / highlights & problems / next-step recommendation, each with numbers."
**Output sample**:
> **Conclusion**: June sales 186k, down about 12% vs. May, mainly dragged by weekday foot traffic.
> **Highlights**: conversion ~44% (620/1400), healthy; weekends contribute steadily.
> **Problems**: ① down 26k vs. prior month; ② returns 8k, ~4.3% of flow, suggest checking the return-concentrated categories.
> **Next step**: ① run a weekday lunchtime traffic campaign; ② pull return details, confirm if concentrated in a certain batch.
**Human check**: I verified the 186/212 MoM and 620/1400 conversion on my calculator myself; the line "weekends steady" is my judgment, not AI-invented.

### Case 2: Design a business dashboard for your own small business
**Input**: "I run a 20-sqm neighborhood bakery, just me and one clerk; everything is in WeChat Pay records and a notebook."
**AI output** (excerpt):
| Metric | How to compute | How often | Warning line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg daily order value | daily flow ÷ daily orders | daily | below 25 RMB, check over-promotion |
| Weekly returning customers | customers who visited ≥2× in a week | weekly | two weeks of decline → member follow-up |
| Spoilage rate | spoilage cost ÷ daily purchase cost | daily | above 8% → adjust daily stock |
**Human gate**: The warning lines I must adjust by my own cost structure — AI's 25 RMB and 8% are only a starting reference, not truth.

### Case 3: Turn the boss's "analyze this" into a structured answer
**Pain point**: The boss drops "analyze this quarter's data" and you don't know where to start.
**AI approach**: "I have [these data]; first tell me which 6 questions to ask for this kind of business, then tell me which column each question needs."
**Result**: AI gives you an "analysis outline + required-data list"; you go collect and calculate accordingly, and the thinking clears up at once.
**Red line**: Don't paste real client lists or employee salary details wholesale into public AI; use "Client A / Employee 1" instead.

## V. Hands-on Task

- **Background**: You are a [role / store owner] with real (or simulated) operational data for this or last month.
- **Input**: A 10–30 row table, **must be masked**: client names → "Client A," employee names → "Clerk 1," no ID numbers / phones / bank cards.
- **Steps**:
  1. First compute the sums and percentages you need in Excel (or by hand) and write them beside.
  2. Use the "Data Summary Template" below to generate a three-part summary, giving AI the numbers you computed — let it only handle expression.
  3. Use the "Dashboard Design Template" to generate a 5-metric dashboard spec for your business.
  4. Check one by one: does each number AI repeats match what you computed? Fix directly if not.
  5. One iteration: "make the conclusion shorter" / "change the recommendation into actions I can execute next week."
- **Expected output**: 1 *Data-Summary Script* + 1 *Business-Analysis Dashboard Spec*.

## VI. Human Review Checklist

- [ ] **Every number was computed by me in Excel or on a calculator**, not reported by AI; I checked each number AI repeated against my own.
- [ ] The three most error-prone types — MoM, YoY, percentages — I recomputed separately.
- [ ] I stated the data definitions clearly ("sales" = tax-inclusive or not, returns deducted or not); AI did not assume on its own.
- [ ] Every "down / up" reason is a real business reason I filled in, not AI's guessed "could be seasonality."
- [ ] The table contains no real client names, phones, ID numbers, bank accounts, or employee salary details.
- [ ] I adjusted the dashboard warning lines to my own cost structure, not copied AI's reference values.
- [ ] I genuinely dare to send this summary to my boss — not a single number in it I can't explain.

## VII. Common Mistakes

1. **Letting AI do arithmetic**: the most frequent and most fatal. Whether AI computes 18.6÷21.2 as -12% or -14%, if you don't check before sending, you get caught in the meeting and lose all trust. **Numbers to Excel, words to AI.**
2. **Not stating column meanings**: dump a pile of numbers, AI reads "return amount" as "sales amount," and the whole conclusion is wrong. Write the data dictionary before pasting.
3. **Accepting AI-invented causes**: "the decline may be intensified market competition" — true for any industry, means nothing. Attribution must be yours; you know the store was under renovation that week.
4. **Piling 20 dashboard metrics**: too many and you watch none. 5–7, each with a warning line and an action, is what actually gets used.
5. **Pasting sensitive data wholesale into public AI**: client lists, employee salaries, unpublished revenue are trade secrets. Mask them or use an internal compliant tool.

## VIII. Homework (Deliverables)

**Deliverables**:
1. A *Data-Summary Script*: three-part (conclusion / highlights & problems / next step), each with numbers, ending with one line "all numbers in this report were computed and verified by me in Excel."
2. A *Business-Analysis Dashboard Spec*: 5–7 metrics, with computation, data source, frequency, warning line, attribution column.
3. A 100-word *My Number-Verification Log*: state which numbers you recomputed and whether you found AI repeating any wrong.

Simulated data is fine, but must be masked, structurally complete, with key facts marked "[TBD]."
