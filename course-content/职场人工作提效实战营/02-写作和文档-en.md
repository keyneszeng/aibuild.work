# Lesson 2 · Writing & Documents → Notice / Weekly Report / Proposal Templates

## I. Learning Goals

- **Knowledge**: Master the "role + background + reader + format + constraints" writing method; understand that "build the skeleton first, then fill in the flesh" is steadier than "writing it all in one go."
- **Skill**: Use AI to generate first drafts of three high-frequency documents (notice, weekly report, proposal), and rewrite them in your role's tone.
- **Output**: A reusable three-piece template set of Notice / Weekly Report / Proposal.

## II. Warm-up Question

> The last time you wrote a weekly report, did you stare at a blank document for over 10 minutes?
> Have you ever sent out a notice that got replies like "didn't get it / missing the time"?

Writing is hard not because "you can't write," but because "you don't know where to start, how formal to be, or how long to make it." This lesson gives you three "fill-in-the-blank" templates.

## III. Core Concepts

### 1. The five parts of a good document-writing Prompt

Referencing Prompt-Engineering-Guide's "Prompt Elements": instruction, context, input data, output indicator, and examples. For working professionals, it comes down to:

- **Role**: Make AI write like a "senior admin / sales manager."
- **Background**: Your company / reason / timeframe.
- **Reader**: Who it's written for (determines tone).
- **Format**: How many paragraphs, how many points, table or bullets.
- **Constraints**: Word count, no fabrication, must include certain info.

### 2. Zero-shot vs. giving examples (Few-shot)

- **Zero-shot**: State the requirement directly — good for general documents.
- **Give examples**: You paste a "previously well-written weekly report" and have AI imitate your style — this is called few-shot, and it matches your taste better.

### 3. Iterative refinement

Unhappy with the first version? Don't rewrite — just say: "make the tone more formal," "delete point 2," "add a risk note." AI edits faster than humans.

## IV. Real Work Scenario Demo

### Case 1: Temporary water-shutoff notice

**Input**: "Tomorrow 9–11am maintenance shutoff, remind everyone to store water."
**AI draft**: Four sections — reason / time / suggestion / contact — with an emoji heading.
**Human check**: Confirm time and building number are correct, then send.

### Case 2: Weekly report from "running log" to "with focus"

**Pain point**: Writing a pile of "what I did," but the boss complains there's no focus.
**AI approach**: Paste this week's items to AI, asking it to "rewrite into three parts — results / in progress / risks & asks — one sentence each."
**Red line**: Weekly reports contain unpublished performance; internal use only, don't send out.

### Case 3: A collaboration proposal framework for a client

**Input**: Product selling points + client's industry.
**AI output**: Background / pain point / our solution / expected benefit / next steps, five-paragraph form.
**Human check**: Benefit numbers must be filled with real data by you; AI must not invent them.

## V. Hands-on Task

- **Background**: You are a [role], and this week you need to send 1 notice + write 1 weekly report + prepare 1 small proposal.
- **Input**: Real or simulated work materials (please mask sensitive info).
- **Steps**:
  1. Use the 3 templates below to generate first drafts respectively.
  2. Do 1 round of iterative editing for each (tone / cutting / supplementing).
  3. Human-verify the key facts.
- **Expected output**: One finished notice, one weekly report, one proposal.

### This Lesson's Prompt Template (Standard Structure, copy one of three)

**Notice template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a clear, tactful administrative colleague
My background is: [company/department] will conduct [matter] on [time], need to notify relevant colleagues
The task I need to complete is: write an internal notice
The target user / reader is: [who] (e.g., all residents / project group members)
Existing materials are as follows: [key info such as water shutoff / meeting / holiday]
Please output: a notice containing reason / time & place / what the other party should do / contact person
Output format: clear sections, bold heading, under 200 words
Constraints: don't fabricate time or place; tone [formal / friendly]; no real names or phone numbers
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

**Weekly report template**

```
You are now playing the role of: my weekly-report partner
My background is: I am a [role], this week's work is as follows [paste items]
The task I need to complete is: turn the running log into a focused weekly report
The target user / reader is: my direct supervisor
Existing materials are as follows: [what I did this week, masked]
Please output: a weekly report in three parts — this week's results / in progress / risks & coordination needed — one sentence per point
Output format: Markdown, about 3 points per section
Constraints: no exaggeration, no fabricated data; unpublished performance internal only
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

**Proposal template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a [industry] proposal consultant with 10 years of experience
My background is: we provide [product/service], target clients are [client type]
The task I need to complete is: write an initial collaboration-proposal framework for a client
The target user / reader is: the potential client's decision-maker
Existing materials are as follows: [our selling points], [client pain points]
Please output: a proposal with background & pain point / our solution / expected benefit / collaboration steps / next step
Output format: five-paragraph form, each with a subheading
Constraints: use [TBD] placeholder for benefit numbers, never invent; no competitor secrets
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

## VI. Human Review Checklist

- [ ] I verified the notice's time, place, and contact person.
- [ ] Weekly-report data are real, not exaggerated, and no unpublished performance leaked.
- [ ] The proposal's benefit / pricing uses a "TBD" placeholder — no AI-fabricated numbers.
- [ ] Tone fits the reader (different for your boss vs. a group chat).
- [ ] All three went through at least one round of iterative editing, not copied straight from the first draft.

## VII. Common Mistakes

1. **No reader distinction**: Writing a weekly report to your boss in "group-chat tone."
2. **Letting AI invent numbers**: The proposal benefit is just made up as "30% improvement." You must supply the numbers yourself.
3. **Asking for too much at once**: Having AI write a 2000-word proposal that comes out hollow. Frame first, then fill.
4. **No iteration**: Abandoning the draft just because it's not smooth. One "make it tighter" often fixes it.
5. **Forgetting to mask**: Writing a real client name into a practice Prompt and sending it to public AI.

## VIII. Homework (Deliverables)

**Deliverable**: One copy each of the Notice + Weekly Report + Proposal three-piece set (simulated data is fine, but structure complete, tone correct, and key facts annotated).
