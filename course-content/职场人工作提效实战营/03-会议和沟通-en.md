# Lesson 3 · Meetings & Communication → Meeting Minutes + Follow-up List

## I. Learning Goals

- **Knowledge**: Understand that the essence of "meeting minutes" is not "writing it down" but "turning what happened into what needs to be done"; master which of the three AI capabilities — information extraction / text summarization / tone rewriting — map to pre-, mid-, and post-meeting stages; know why "owner + deadline" are the two columns you can least afford to skip.
- **Skill**: Turn a messy meeting transcript, chat log, or incoming email into a clearly structured, forwardable minutes or reply; use "give examples (Few-shot)" to make AI imitate your company's existing minutes style.
- **Output**: A "Standard Meeting-Minutes Template" + a "clear cross-department communication reply" you can use in this week's meetings.

> This lesson's methods reference Prompt-Engineering-Guide's real frameworks — "Text Summarization," "Information Extraction," and "Prompt Elements (instruction / context / input / output indicator / examples)" — translated into actions a working professional can use directly.

## II. Warm-up Question

> From last week's meeting, how many points did you note down? After it ended, how many items were actually followed up and completed on time?
> Have you ever had this experience: the other party sends a 300-character, un-paragraphed WeChat/email with the key point buried in the third line, and after reading it twice you still don't know "what exactly do they want me to do"?

Most professionals don't "not know how to meet" — they "lose it after the meeting": incomplete notes, unclear logic, inaccurate relaying. This lesson has AI do three things for you: **think through what to discuss before the meeting, turn talk into a list during/after, and turn vague external communication into clear communication.**

## III. Core Concepts

### 1. Meeting minutes = Information Extraction

The most valuable output of a meeting isn't "who said what," but "who, by when, must deliver what." This is exactly AI's strength — "information extraction": you give it source text, and it finds the elements "decision / to-do / owner / deadline / risk" and lists them in a table. You add one human check — are the names right, are the times wrong.

### 2. Three kinds of meetings, three AI uses

- **Meetings with recording/transcript text** (most common): paste the transcript to AI and have it output "decisions + action-item table."
- **Meetings with only scattered chats** (WeChat groups, DingTalk): paste the chat log to AI and have it summarize "consensus reached" and "to-dos."
- **Pure mental prep**: before the meeting, have AI list "5 questions to confirm in this meeting" — walk in with the list, double the efficiency.

### 3. The "golden five columns" of structured minutes

Actionable minutes need at least five columns: **item / owner / deadline / current status / risk or coordination needed**. Of these, "owner" and "deadline" are the least skippable — minutes without these two columns equal no meeting at all.

### 4. External communication = Tone Rewriting

Hand the other party's vague words, or what you want to say but fear offending, to AI to rewrite into a "clear, tactful, no-blame" version. This is the combo of "instruction + reader": tell AI who the reader is and what tone you want, and it removes the sting from your words.

### 5. Give examples (Few-shot) to imitate your style

Every company's minutes style differs. Paste an old minutes your boss was happy with as an example and say "write in this style," and the output fits your taste far better. This is called few-shot (example prompting), more accurate than describing the style from scratch.

## IV. Real Work Scenario Demo

### Case 1: Weekly-meeting minutes from "running log" to "action table"

**Source material** (transcript excerpt): "That client follow-up from last week still isn't done, Xiao Wang said this week, and then that poster is also late, design says waiting for assets, and next month's budget needs to be reported early…"
**AI approach**: Paste the text, ask it to "extract action items, output in four columns: item / owner / deadline / status."
**Output sample**:

| Item | Owner | Deadline | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client follow-up wrap-up | Xiao Wang | This Friday | In progress |
| Poster production | Design (awaiting assets) | This Wed | Blocked: awaiting assets |
| Next-month budget report | Me | 25th of month | Not started |

**Human check**: Confirm "Xiao Wang" and "Design" are correct names/roles, and the deadlines match your memory, before sending to the group.

### Case 2: Rewrite a vague counterpart email into a clear reply

**Counterpart's original**: "You guys figure out how to handle that thing, anyway ASAP, oh and the fee we mentioned before isn't quite right either."
**AI approach**: "Help me rewrite this reply into a professional, unambiguous version that moves the other party, and list 2 questions I need to confirm with them."
**Output sample**: "Regarding project progress, we suggest confirming plan A or B before this Wednesday; we also need you to confirm two points: (1) which specific payment does the fee discrepancy refer to? (2) does 'ASAP' mean within this week? Upon confirmation we will start immediately."
**Human check**: Fee amounts and plan names must be filled with real info by you; AI doesn't invent numbers.

### Case 3: Report upward so "the boss gets it in 3 minutes"

**Pain point**: You have 8 things in hand; the boss only wants to know "where's the progress, any blockers, what do you need from me."
**AI approach**: Paste the messy progress, ask it to "rewrite as 'conclusion first + 3 key points + 1 ask,' one sentence each."
**Red line**: Contains unpublished performance / personnel changes — internal only, don't send out.

## V. Hands-on Task

- **Background**: You are a [role], with at least 1 meeting / 1 cross-department written communication this week.
- **Input**: A meeting transcript or a vague message from the other party (please mask, use "a client / Xiao Wang" instead of real names).
- **Steps**:
  1. Use the "minutes template" below to generate a structured action table.
  2. Use the "communication reply template" to rewrite a vague message into a clear reply.
  3. Do 1 round of iteration each: "make the tone more formal," "bold the deadlines."
  4. Human-verify names, times, amounts.
- **Expected output**: 1 minutes + 1 communication reply.

### This Lesson's Prompt Template (Standard Structure)

**Meeting-minutes template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a senior admin / project assistant skilled at turning meeting recordings into actionable to-do lists
My background is: I am a [role], just finished a [weekly / project / cross-department meeting], with transcript text but don't want to organize it line by line myself
The task I need to complete is: turn the meeting content into a structured work product
The target user / reader is: [attending colleagues / my boss]
Existing materials are as follows: [paste transcript text or chat log, masked]
Please output: ① one-sentence meeting conclusion; ② action-item table with columns item / owner / deadline / status / risk; ③ items to escalate to the boss for decision
Output format: Markdown, conclusion first then table
Constraints: don't fabricate names or times; use [TBD] placeholder for names if the source is unclear; no real client names or phone numbers
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

**Communication-reply template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a tactful colleague who gets things resolved
My background is: the other party sent a vague message [paste], I need to reply but don't want to seem vague or evasive
The task I need to complete is: rewrite my reply into a clear, professional version that pushes the other party to give definite info
The target user / reader is: [counterpart's role, e.g., client / partner / superior]
Existing materials are as follows: [counterpart's original words] + [result I want to achieve]
Please output: ① the full rewritten reply; ② a list of 2–3 key questions I need to confirm with the counterpart
Output format: reply body + question list, two sections
Constraints: don't fabricate fees / times etc.; tone [professional / friendly]; don't expose internal secrets
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

## VI. Human Review Checklist

- [ ] Every "owner" in the minutes is a real person/role — no name AI conjured from nothing.
- [ ] Every "deadline" matches my memory or calendar — no AI-fabricated dates.
- [ ] I genuinely intend to send out and follow up the action table, not just do the homework and drop it.
- [ ] Fees, plan names, and time points in the reply are real info I filled in.
- [ ] Minutes with unpublished info were sent internally only, not mistakenly to an external group.

## VII. Common Mistakes

1. **Treating AI like a voice recorder**: thinking pasting it in is fully automatic. AI does "organizing"; the raw transcript may drop words, and you must supply key conclusions.
2. **Missing owner/time**: minutes only say "do X" with no one claiming it and no deadline — equal to not writing it.
3. **Copying without adjusting tone**: AI's reply is too stiff/too soft, doesn't fit your relationship with the other party, and sending it is awkward.
4. **Forgetting to mask**: dropping a chat log with real client names into public AI for minutes.
5. **No iteration**: redoing from scratch just because the first version's format is off; actually saying "add a risk column" is ten times faster than rewriting.

## VIII. Homework (Deliverables)

**Deliverable**: ① A real or simulated "Structured Minutes" (with conclusion + action table + escalation items); ② a "Clear Cross-Department Reply" (targeting a vague message). Simulated data is fine, but structure complete and key facts annotated with "[TBD]."
