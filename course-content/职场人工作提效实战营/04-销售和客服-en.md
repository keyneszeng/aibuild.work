# Lesson 4 · Sales & Customer Service → Scripts / Objection Handling / Client Follow-up

## I. Learning Goals

- **Knowledge**: Understand that "scripts" aren't rote-memorized but generated live from "scenario + audience"; master "give examples (Few-shot)" to teach AI your winning-script style; understand that "objection handling" can be broken into four steps — empathize → clarify → evidence → advance — using "Chain-of-Thought"; know why AI must never promise price, delivery, or policy on your behalf.
- **Skill**: Generate ice-breaking / follow-up / closing scripts for any sales scenario; produce tactful responses to common objections like "too expensive" / "let me think" / "competitor is cheaper"; hand your product FAQ / policy docs to AI so it answers customer questions based on "your materials" (a Retrieval-Augmented Generation / RAG approach) rather than making things up.
- **Output**: A "Sales Script Library" (ice-breaking, follow-up, 3 objection types) + a "Customer-Service Standard Reply + Complaint-Soothing Template."

> This lesson's methods reference Prompt-Engineering-Guide's real frameworks — "Few-shot," "Chain-of-Thought," "Classification," "RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)," "Responsible AI" — plus the RAG idea from LLM Universe of "turning materials into a queryable knowledge base."

## II. Warm-up Question

> When a client says "too expensive," what's your usual next line? Do you freeze, or is it different every time, relying on inspiration?
> When a client asks a question you've answered 100 times ("can you issue an invoice?" "how long to ship?"), are you hand-typing the 101st?
> With 40 WeChat clients, who needs a follow-up, who last said "let me think" — can your brain still hold it all?

Sales and customer service are "script-intensive + repetition-intensive" roles. AI doesn't sell for you; it turns your "inspiration-type scripts" into an "on-call ammunition library," and turns "repetitive Q&A" into "capture once, reuse forever."

## III. Core Concepts

### 1. Script = scenario × audience, generated live

Good scripts have no standard answer, only "right person + right matter + right tone." You give AI three things — **client type, communication stage, desired vibe** — and it generates several versions for you to pick. More flexible than memorizing, steadier than improvising.

### 2. Give examples (Few-shot): let AI learn your "winning flavor"

Paste a real winning chat log of yours (masked) to AI and say "in this tone and logic, write a new ice-breaking script." AI will imitate your rhythm instead of handing you a strange mass template. This is the core value of few-shot: **use your good examples to teach AI your good.**

### 3. Objection handling = Chain-of-Thought

When a client says "too expensive," don't rush to throw a discount. Have AI reason in four steps: **① empathize (I get that the budget must be calculated) ② clarify (who are you comparing with / total price or monthly fee?) ③ evidence (our differentiated value / case) ④ advance (give you a small trial / plan first)**. Make it "go step by step" — more professional than a one-line discount.

### 4. Customer-service standard reply = based on your materials (RAG approach)

What customer service fears most is AI "inventing company policy." The right way: paste your "Product FAQ" / "Return & Exchange Policy" into the conversation (or upload to a tool that supports a knowledge base), and tell AI "answer only based on the materials I gave; if the materials don't cover it, honestly say 'I'll confirm and get back to you.'" This is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) — **let AI answer based on your facts, not make them up from memory.**

### 5. Client tiering & emotion recognition = Classification

Paste client messages to AI and have it tag "high/medium/low intent" and "emotion: calm / dissatisfied / angry," so you prioritize the high-heat ones. This is the everyday use of classification tasks.

### 6. Red line: AI doesn't promise for you

Price, discount, delivery time, warranty policy — these **only you can give**; AI must not invent them. Any specific promise in a script uses a "[TBD by me]" placeholder.

## IV. Real Work Scenario Demo

### Case 1: First-touch ice-breaking script (masked)

**Background**: Added a new potential client on WeChat; need a non-awkward opener.
**AI output** (excerpt): "Hi Manager Wang, I'm [nickname] in [industry]. Saw your company is doing [business]; we just helped a similar company cut [pain point] by 30%. If convenient, may I send you a 1-page case for reference?"
**Human check**: The 30% is your real case data — don't let AI invent it; use the real surname in the address.

### Case 2: "Too expensive" objection handling

**AI four steps**: ① "Totally understand, the budget surely needs to be clear" ② "Just to confirm, are you comparing with which vendor's plan? More concerned about total price or long-term cost?" ③ "Our difference is [three points]; client XX achieved [real result] after use" ④ "How about I open a [trial / small order] first, so you can feel it before deciding?"
**Red line**: The "real result" in step ③ must be filled by you; AI doesn't invent.

### Case 3: Customer-service complaint soothing

**Background**: A client blows up over slow shipping.
**AI output**: ① acknowledge emotion first — "truly sorry for the long wait" ② give facts — "your order is currently at step X, expected to ship Y" ③ give compensation plan — "[TBD by me]" ④ leave follow-up — "I'll notify you the moment it ships."
**Key**: Facts and compensation must both be filled with real info by you.

## V. Hands-on Task

- **Background**: You are a [sales / CS], with real or simulated client communication scenarios.
- **Input**: 1 past script of yours (masked) or 1 real objection, 1 common client question.
- **Steps**:
  1. Use the "script generation template" to produce 1 version each of ice-breaking + follow-up (use your example for few-shot).
  2. Use the "objection handling template" to handle "too expensive" and "let me think."
  3. Use the "customer-service reply template" to answer 1 client question based on your pasted FAQ.
  4. Human-verify all prices / policies / data are "[TBD by me]" or real values.
- **Expected output**: 1 script library + 2 objection responses + 1 customer-service reply.

### This Lesson's Prompt Template (Standard Structure)

**Script generation template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a [industry] salesperson with 5 years of experience, down-to-earth, not sleazy
My background is: I am a [role], reaching [client type], currently at the [ice-breaking / follow-up / closing] stage
The task I need to complete is: write a WeChat / phone opening script
The target user / reader is: [client role and pain point]
Existing materials are as follows: [my product selling points] + [a past winning script of mine (masked) as example]
Please output: 2 versions of the script, one leaning [professional] and one [friendly], each under 80 words
Output format: two versions side by side, labeled with suitable timing
Constraints: don't fabricate client case data; price/policy use [TBD by me]; no real client names
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

**Objection handling template**

```
You are now playing the role of: a sales consultant skilled at dissolving client concerns
My background is: the client says "[too expensive / let me think / competitor cheaper]", I don't want to cut price directly
The task I need to complete is: respond following the four steps empathize → clarify → evidence → advance
The target user / reader is: this client
Existing materials are as follows: [our differentiated value] + [one real client result, masked]
Please output: four-step script, one sentence per step; end with 1 non-price alternative (e.g., trial)
Output format: four numbered steps + alternative
Constraints: evidence-step data must be filled by me with [real data], AI doesn't invent; don't promise discounts I'm not authorized to give
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

**Customer-service reply template (based on materials / RAG approach)**

```
You are now playing the role of: a patient, professional customer-service rep
My background is: the client asks "[question]", I have standard materials to reference
The task I need to complete is: based on the materials, give an accurate, emotion-soothing reply
The target user / reader is: this client
Existing materials are as follows: [paste the relevant paragraphs of your FAQ / policy doc]
Please output: ① one sentence of empathy first; ② a facts-based answer from the materials; ③ next-step action
Output format: three-paragraph form, total under 120 words
Constraints: answer only based on the materials I gave; if not covered, clearly say "I'll confirm and get back to you" — never fabricate policy / time / compensation
First ask me 3 key questions; if the information is sufficient, start directly.
Please output a version directly usable in work scenarios, with a checklist attached
```

## VI. Human Review Checklist

- [ ] Client cases/data in the scripts are real or clearly "[TBD]," with no AI-fabricated percentages.
- [ ] The "evidence" in objection-handling step ③ comes from my real business, not invented by AI.
- [ ] Customer-service reply facts (shipping / returns / invoices) match my policy doc.
- [ ] Anything involving price, discount, or delivery promises was filled by me; AI didn't promise on its own.
- [ ] No real client names, phone numbers, or order numbers fed to public AI.

## VII. Common Mistakes

1. **Letting AI invent cases**: "50% improvement" was made up by AI. Case data must come from you.
2. **Cutting price the moment an objection appears**: AI helps you advance in four steps — don't skip empathy/clarify and throw a discount.
3. **Letting AI freelance the policy**: asking "can I return it?" without pasting materials — AI may promise recklessly. Always use the RAG approach: give materials, limit scope.
4. **Too templated scripts**: not feeding your own examples yields generic pitches. Use few-shot to learn your flavor.
5. **Forgetting to mask**: dropping client chat logs as-is into public AI.
