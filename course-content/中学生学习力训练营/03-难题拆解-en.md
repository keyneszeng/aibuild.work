# 03 · Breaking Down Hard Problems → Step-by-Step Mistake Card

## Learning Goals

1. Master the "break a big problem into small steps" decomposition mindset (similar to the Chain-of-Thought idea).
2. Use AI as a "step-by-step sparring partner": it gives one hint, you do one step — instead of giving the whole solution.
3. Build a "step-by-step mistake card" tool, turning every wrong problem into a reusable learning asset.
4. Stick to "guided" decomposition: AI doesn't calculate for you, it only helps you figure out the next step.

---

## Warm-up Question

> You stare at a hard geometry final problem for 10 minutes with no idea. What should you do first?
> A. Immediately ask AI: "What's the answer to this problem?"
> B. Ask AI: "What known conditions can I confirm first for this problem? Where do I usually start? Guide me, don't solve it directly."
>
> Those who choose B are slower, but next time they meet a similar problem, they'll be able to break it down themselves.

## Core Concepts

### 1. Why Decomposition Works
Big hard problems make people give up because they are "too big, too vague." Once broken into 3–5 small steps, each step becomes doable. This is also a common AI reasoning method (step-by-step thinking).

### 2. A General Framework for Decomposition
- **Given conditions**: What does the problem give you?
- **What is the goal**: What to prove / find?
- **Middle bridge**: From known to goal, what steps usually lie in between?
- **Sticking-point location**: Exactly which step am I stuck on?
- **Next action**: What can I try first for this step?

### 3. AI's Correct Role in Decomposition
AI should not "solve it all at once," but should:
- Help you list the given conditions (you verify)
- Hint "usually you can think about the next step this way" (you decide whether to adopt it)
- You do one step, it looks at it, then gives the next hint

### 4. The Value of the Step-by-Step Mistake Card
A mistake is not "wrong and done" — it's "at which step did my thinking break." The step-by-step card marks the break point so you can avoid it next time.

## Learning Scenario Example

### Math: a comprehensive function problem
- **Wrong use**: "Send me the answer to this function problem." → Copy it, still can't do it next time.
- **Right use**: "In this problem I need to find where the parabola intersects the x-axis. Please remind me: to find the intersection, what should y usually be set equal to? Guide me, I'll calculate it myself." → AI hints "set y=0," you compute the intersection and continue to the next step.

### Physics: a force-analysis problem
- **Wrong use**: Ask directly for the final acceleration.
- **Right use**: "I always miss friction force in the force analysis of an object on an incline. Please guide me to draw it step by step in the order 'gravity first, then normal force, then friction,' don't give the result directly."

### History: a document-analysis problem
- **Wrong use**: "What's the main point of this passage? Just say it."
- **Right use**: "This passage is about the Self-Strengthening Movement. Please break it into three steps 'background — measures — results,' and for each step give me one guiding question so I can find the answer in the original text myself."

## Correct vs. Incorrect Usage

| | ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What AI gives | Full solution | Single-step hint |
| What you do | Copy-paste | Derive each step yourself |
| Mistake handling | Cross it out and redo | Mark the break point, make a step card |
| Gain | 0 | Transferable decomposition skill |

## Hands-on Task

1. Find a problem you got wrong recently (any subject).
2. Use the "decomposition framework" above to break it into 3–5 steps, writing it on a "step-by-step mistake card."
3. Take the "sticking point" from the card and ask AI with a guided question: "I'm stuck at step ___, please give me only one hint, don't solve it."
4. Complete that step yourself, and on the card note "why I was wrong before."

---

## Prompt Template (for middle/high school students)

```
I am a [grade ______] student, working on a [subject] problem.
The problem is: [paste problem ______].
What I've already figured out: [completed steps ______].
I'm stuck at: [specific sticking point ______].
Please act as a coach and give me only a hint for the [next step], guiding me to understand, without giving the final answer or the full solution directly.
After I work it out, I'll tell you the result, and you'll judge what my next step should be.
```

> Red line: Only ever ask for "the next step"; never ask for the full solution at once.

---

## Human Review Checklist (student self-check)

- [ ] I broke the hard problem into at least 3 clear steps.
- [ ] AI only gave "the next-step hint," not solving it for me.
- [ ] I completed every step and can say what each step does.
- [ ] I marked the "sticking point" and "why I was wrong before."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Ask for the whole thing the moment you're stuck**: asking AI for the full solution wastes the decomposition practice.
2. **Not writing the card**: thinking it through in your head without writing it down — you'll make the same mistake next time.
3. **Copy without understanding**: AI gives a hint, you copy the hint as the answer without truly understanding.
4. **No review of mistakes**: after making the card you throw it away, never looking back periodically.

---

## Homework (Deliverables)

1. Complete at least 2 "step-by-step mistake cards" (1 each for different subjects), including: given conditions, goal, steps, sticking point, cause of error.
2. Using the guided template, actually ask AI once about the sticking point of one card; record its hint and your completion.
3. One week later, look back at these two cards and write "Would I still get this problem wrong now?"

## Section Summary & Extension

Decomposition turns an "undoable big problem" into "doable small steps." The core trick is: **Only ever ask AI for "the next step," never the full solution at once.** Every step you do is you growing your own ability; AI is just the coach giving you a hand. The value of the step-by-step mistake card is marking "where did I go wrong" as a signpost, so you actively avoid it next time.

### Extension: Draw a "Mind Map"
When a problem has more than 4 steps, it's recommended to draw a mind map on paper: write the given conditions on the left, draw arrows connecting each derivation step in the middle, and write the goal on the right. The step you're stuck on is usually where the arrow breaks. Circle the break point and ask AI — far more efficient than throwing the whole problem at it.

### One Sentence for Parents/Teachers
Don't rush to see "did the child get it right"; first see "did they break it down correctly." A problem done wrong but broken down sensibly is more valuable than one guessed right. Writing a line like "great thinking on this step" on the card protects learning motivation better than a score.
