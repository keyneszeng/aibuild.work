# 04 · English & Writing Training → Revision Log

## Learning Goals

1. Use AI as a "writing coach": revise your essay, point out problems, give actionable suggestions — not rewrite it for you.
2. Build the habit of a "revision log": draft → feedback → second draft, so you can see your own progress.
3. Improve English expression and Chinese writing awareness of logic, word choice, and structure.
4. Hold the red line: AI improves "how to write better," but the final text must come from you.

---

## Warm-up Question

> You wrote an English short essay and the grammar is a bit messy. What would you do?
> A. Throw the whole thing at AI: "Rewrite it for me." → It becomes AI's writing; you didn't practice.
> B. Throw it at AI: "Please only mark the 3 errors that most hurt my score, explain why each is wrong, and let me fix them myself." → You practiced, understood, and now can do it.
>
> Writing ability is "revised" into being, not "generated" into being.

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. AI's Good Role in Writing
- **Reviewer**: Point out grammar, logic, and structure problems.
- **Explainer**: Explain "why this is wrong here."
- **Sparring partner**: Brainstorm arguments and examples with you.
- **Not a ghostwriter**: Not writing the whole essay for you.

### 2. The Three-Column Structure of a Revision Log
| Original sentence/paragraph | Problem AI points out | My fix + why |
This way you can see "from what to what, and why."

### 3. Common Levers in English Writing
- Tense agreement, subject-verb agreement
- Precise word choice (don't overuse very / good / big)
- Connectors (however / therefore / for example)
- Sentence variety (mix long and short sentences)

### 4. Same for Chinese Writing
Structure (thesis–argument–restatement), evidence support, avoid colloquialism, paragraph transitions.

---

## Learning Scenario Example

### English essay: My Weekend (120 words)
- **Wrong use**: "Write my 120-word My Weekend for me." → Submit directly; the teacher sees at a glance it's not yours.
- **Right use**: Paste your own draft and ask "Find 3 grammar errors and explain them; don't rewrite, I'll fix them myself." After you fix them, ask "Can the flow of this version be better? Give 1 suggestion."

### Chinese argumentative essay: on "Perseverance"
- **Wrong use**: Let AI generate the whole essay.
- **Right use**: You write a draft, ask "Are my arguments supported by examples? The logic in paragraph 2 skips a step — please point out the gap so I can fill it myself."

## Correct vs. Incorrect Usage

| | ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input | The topic | Your own draft |
| AI output | A brand-new article | Problem marking + explanation |
| What you do | Copy and submit | Fix yourself, record it |
| Skill gain | None | Visible progress |

## Hands-on Task

1. Write a 100–150-word English short essay (or a 300-character Chinese mini-essay) yourself, any topic.
2. Paste the draft to AI and ask for feedback in the mode "only mark 3 errors + explain."
3. Based on the feedback, revise into a second draft and fill in the three-column "revision log."
4. Show the second draft to AI once more to confirm whether obvious problems remain (still only ask for suggestions, don't let anyone rewrite).

---

## Prompt Template (for middle/high school students)

```
I am a [grade ______] student, just wrote a [English/Chinese] essay on the topic [______].
Below is my original draft: [paste ______].
Please do not rewrite it for me. Please:
1. Only mark [3] issues that most hurt the score (grammar/logic/structure);
2. For each, explain "why this is a problem";
3. Give me a direction I can fix myself, without giving ready-made sentences.
I want to revise it myself and record the revision process.
```

> Red line: AI doesn't write for you; the final text is 100% from your hand.

---

## Human Review Checklist (student self-check)

- [ ] The original draft is my own writing, no AI ghostwriting.
- [ ] AI only pointed out problems and explained, without rewriting.
- [ ] I completed the second draft and filled the three-column revision log.
- [ ] I can say "which type of error I fixed correctly."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Letting someone ghostwrite**: passing AI's output off as your own homework is academic misconduct.
2. **Fixing without understanding**: following AI's fix but unable to say why.
3. **Asking for too much feedback at once**: marking 10 issues, but then can't take them all in.
4. **Not recording**: throwing it away after fixing, so progress is invisible and hard to review.

2. Actually operate once with the guided template and submit the log table.
3. Write one sentence: Which type of error did this revision help you improve the most?

## Section Summary & Extension

Writing ability is "revised" into being. AI's best role is reviewer and explainer: it points out problems and explains the reasons; you do the fixing and record why. The three-column revision log (original / problem / my fix) makes progress visible, and lets teachers and parents see at a glance that this is your own growth, not AI's output.

### Shared Principles of English and Chinese Writing
Good writing in any language follows three rules:
1. **One clear idea**: one paragraph says one thing.
2. **Support with examples**: after stating a point, immediately give evidence or a story.
3. **Natural connections**: link sentences with "because / but / for example / so."
Use these three as a checklist: self-review once, then hand it to AI to catch gaps — best efficiency.

### One Sentence for Parents/Teachers
When collecting homework, focus on the "revision log" rather than just the final draft. The child's own line in the log — "I fixed the tense error" — shows real progress better than a perfect-score essay. Encourage keeping a semester of revision logs; it becomes a writing-growth portfolio.

### Quick Self-Check Card (30 seconds before submitting)
- Did I write this myself? Yes → continue.
- Did AI only mark problems, not write for me? Yes → continue.
- Can I say which type of error I fixed? Yes → submit with confidence.
If any of the three is "no," do that step first before submitting.
- Tip: Re-read your writing a day later; you'll often find new problems. This is the "cooling effect," a secret weapon of good writers.
