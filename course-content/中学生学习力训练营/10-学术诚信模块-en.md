# 10 · Academic Integrity Module (Fact-Checking / Citing Sources / Avoiding Plagiarism)

> This module is the training camp's **hard red line**, paired with Lesson 06 (Judging True from False) but focused on "academic integrity":
> what counts as plagiarism, how to cite correctly, how to hold the honesty bottom line in the AI age.
> Whether school, institution, or self-study at home, this module's content must be implemented.

---

## I. Why This Lesson
AI makes "writing a decent passage" extremely easy, and also makes it easier for students to cross the line unintentionally:
- Submitting AI-generated text as if self-written (concealment + plagiarism);
- Directly copying web / encyclopedia without citation (theft);
- Citing references AI fabricated (fabrication);
- Using AI to take exams / do homework for you (academic fraud).

**Academic integrity is not a skill of "not getting caught," but a bottom line of "being responsible for your own learning."**
Using AI to assist learning is totally OK; using AI to replace learning, conceal its use, or plagiarize others is not.

---

## II. Core Concepts

### 1. What is "Plagiarism"
Submitting content you didn't originally create (text, ideas, data, images) as your own without citing the source is plagiarism. Including:
- Copying AI / web / classmate homework in whole passages;
- Changing only a few words and calling it your own ("spin-plagiarism" also counts);
- Translating someone's article without citation.

### 2. What is "Reasonable Use of AI"
✅ Use AI to explain concepts, give ideas, give revision feedback, generate practice problems;
✅ Form your own expression with AI's help, and truly understand it;
✅ **Note "this homework was completed with AI assistance"** in the assignment / report.
❌ Let AI write the full answer / essay / code and you only copy;
❌ Conceal the use of AI;
❌ Cite references AI fabricated.

### 3. Citation basics
When you use someone's words, data, or ideas, cite: who said it, where from, what year.
- Book / article: Author, *Title*, publisher / journal, year.
- Webpage: Site name, article title, URL, access date.
- Data: Publishing body, data name, year.
> Note: references AI gives are often **non-existent** — you must find the original yourself to cite.

### 4. "Citability" of AI content
AI-generated content usually **should not be cited directly as an authoritative source**. Correct approach:
- Treat AI as "inspiration / draft"; ultimately support your argument with authoritative sources;
- If you must mention AI assistance, write "this was drafted with AI tool assistance; key facts were checked."

---

## III. Right vs. Wrong Use

| Scenario | ❌ Dishonest | ✅ Honest approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing essay | AI writes the whole piece, submit as own | Write yourself, AI gives feedback, note assistance |
| Using data | Copy AI's fake data | Use authoritative source, mark origin |
| Citing | Cite AI's made-up book | Find the real source yourself then cite |
| Doing homework | Let AI do it | Complete yourself, AI only tutors |
| Submitting | Conceal AI use | Honestly note AI assistance |

## IV. Concrete Methods & Scripts

### Method 1: Three-step self-check (must do before submitting)
1. **Cited the source?** For others' words / data / ideas, did you mark the origin?
2. **Do I really get it?** Can I explain this passage without AI? Can't = haven't learned yet.
3. **Said I used AI?** If the assignment requires noting assistance, did I note it?

### Method 2: Citation template scripts
- Chinese assignment: "This assignment was completed with AI learning-assistant help for idea organization and language polishing; key facts were checked, and the core content is original to me."
- Citing a sentence: "As XXX (year) pointed out, … (source)."
- Using data: "According to XXX body (year), … (source link)."

### Method 3: Avoid "spin-plagiarism"
Don't just swap synonyms. Correct approach:
- Close the original, rewrite in your own words;
- After writing, compare to ensure structure and expression are yours;
- If you borrowed someone's framework, clearly acknowledge / mark it.

### Method 4: Reference verification flow
1. AI gives a "reference" → 2. search the title in an academic DB / library → 3. cite only after finding the original → 4. if not found, **delete, don't cite**.

## V. Real Learning-scenario Cases

### Case: English essay
- ❌ AI writes 120 words, submit directly. Teacher finds vocabulary far above usual level, classifies as ghostwriting.
- ✅ Write a draft yourself; AI marks 3 errors and explains; you revise; note at end "AI assisted language polish, content original to me."

### Case: History short essay
- ❌ Copy three encyclopedia paragraphs without citation, pass off as own analysis.
- ✅ Read primary sources yourself, write the viewpoint; cite "p. X of *XXX*" where referenced; data from textbook / authoritative chronology.

### Case: Science lab report
- ❌ AI fabricates the conclusion "experimental data matches expectations" when no experiment was done.
- ✅ Record real data yourself; AI only helps analyze "why the trend is so"; conclusion written by you.

## VI. Hands-on Task
1. Take a previous assignment of yours and do an "integrity scan": any uncited content? Any AI-ghostwriting trace?
2. Use the "three-step self-check" item by item; write down improvement actions.
3. Add a permanent clause to your User Manual (08): "must do the three-step integrity self-check before submitting."

---

## VII. Prompt Template (integrity version)
```
I'm writing [subject / assignment: ______].
Please help me check: among the content below, what might count as "uncited source" or "looks like AI ghostwriting"?
[paste my text: ______]
Only give risk hints, don't rewrite for me; I will complete citations myself and ensure originality.
```
> Red line: AI can hint risks, can't whitewash for you; you are ultimately responsible for integrity.

---

## VIII. Human Review Checklist (self-check)
- [ ] The content I submit, the core is completed by me.
- [ ] For others' words / data / ideas, I marked the source.
- [ ] I didn't cite any reference I hadn't verified myself.
- [ ] If required, I noted AI assistance.
- [ ] I can explain the content without AI (truly understand).

---

## IX. Common Mistakes
1. **"Only changed a few words"**: think it's not copying, but it's still plagiarism.
2. **Concealing AI use**: not noting it equals deception.
3. **Citing fake references**: using AI's made-up books, fabrication.
4. **Ghostwriting / exam-taking**: the most serious academic fraud, heaviest consequences.

---

## X. Homework
1. Write an "Academic Integrity Pledge" (3–5 clauses, including: no ghostwriting, note use, check first).
2. Complete one "integrity scan": pick a past assignment, find and fix 1 improvable point.
3. Post the three-step integrity self-check in your study area as a fixed action before every submission.
