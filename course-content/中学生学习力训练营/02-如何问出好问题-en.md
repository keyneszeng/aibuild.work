# 02 · How to Ask Good Questions → Subject Question Templates

## Learning Goals

1. Understand that "question quality determines answer quality" — a vague question only gets a useless answer.
2. Master the four elements of structured questioning: context, goal, constraints, role.
3. Build subject-specific question template cards (math / English / history / physics, etc.).
4. Learn to embed "guided" requirements in your questions so AI doesn't give the answer directly.

## Warm-up Question

> Xiaoming asks AI: "Help me write an essay about environmental protection."
> Xiaohong asks AI: "I'm a 8th-grader. I need to write a 600-word argumentative essay with the thesis 'garbage sorting starts with small things.' Please first give me 3 arguments with corresponding real-life examples. Don't write the whole thing — I want to write it myself."
>
> Whose result is more useful? Why? — **A good question is the first step to learning well.**

## Core Concepts

### 1. Why Questioning Matters So Much
Large language models "follow along with what you say." The clearer you are, the more they can help; if you're vague, they can only guess, and the result is often useless.

### 2. The Four Elements of a Good Question (STAR Method)
- **Situation (background)**: Who am I, what grade, what am I studying.
- **Task**: What do I want (thinking / feedback / explanation / practice).
- **Action (constraints)**: Length, style, don't give the answer directly, etc.
- **Role**: Have AI play "coach / teacher / sparring partner."

### 3. Shift from "Want the answer" to "Want understanding"
Add to your question: **"Please guide me to understand, don't give the final answer directly — I want to do it myself."**

### 4. Iterative Questioning
First round: ask for a framework. Second round: ask for hints. Third round: ask for feedback — a good question is "asked out" step by step, not in one shot.

## Learning Scenario Example

### Math mistake
- Vague: "How do I do this function problem?"
- Good question: "I'm a 10th-grader. I'm stuck on the step 'how to complete the square' in this quadratic-function problem. Please only hint at the idea of completing the square and guide me to do it myself; don't give the full solution."

### English essay
- Vague: "Help me revise this essay."
- Good question: "I'm a 9th-grader. Below is my 120-word English short essay. Please point out the 3 grammar/word-choice issues that most hurt my score, and explain why each is wrong, so I can fix them myself. Don't rewrite."

### History material
- Vague: "Tell me about the Taiping Rebellion."
- Good question: "I'm previewing the Taiping Rebellion. The textbook says it was 'anti-feudal and anti-aggression.' Please list 3 specific historical facts that support this evaluation, and mark which one is most disputed and why."

### Physics concept
- Vague: "What is Newton's Second Law?"
- Good question: "Using the example of 'pushing a shopping cart,' I keep getting stuck on F=ma at 'the bigger the mass, the harder to accelerate.' Please guide me with this example; don't just recite the formula."

## Correct vs. Incorrect Usage

| | ❌ Incorrect question | ✅ Correct question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information | No background, no goal | Has grade, has specific sticking point |
| Expectation | Want a finished product | Want thinking/feedback/guidance |
| Usability of result | Vague, unusable | Precise, can continue working |
| Learning happens | No | Inside you |

## Hands-on Task

1. Pick a point you didn't understand from a recent assignment in one of your subjects.
2. First write a "vague question," then rewrite it as a "STAR good question."
3. Enter your rewritten question into AI and check whether what it gives you is actually usable; if not, iterate one more round.
4. Copy your final question into your "subject question template card."

## Prompt Template (for middle/high school students)

```
I am a [grade ______] student, studying [subject/topic ______].
I'm currently stuck on: [specific sticking point ______].
Please play the role of my [subject] coach, guide me to understand, and don't give the final answer directly.
I'd like you to:
1. First confirm where I'm confused;
2. Give 1–2 hints to let me move forward myself;
3. After I answer, judge whether to give more clues;
4. Finally test me with 1 question to see if I truly understand.
```

> Red line: Every subject template carries "guide me to understand, don't give the final answer directly."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Question too short and vague**: "Help me study math" — AI has no way to start.
2. **Asking for too much at once**: Wanting explanation + essay + problems all at once, so everything stays shallow.
3. **Forgetting to say "don't give the answer"**: AI handily gives a finished product, and you copy it again.
4. **Not iterating**: Giving up after the first try doesn't work, when actually rephrasing the question would fix it.

## Section Summary & Extension

A good question = clear background + clear goal + reasonable constraints + suitable role. Most "AI answers the wrong thing" cases root in "I asked unclearly." Treat questioning as a practicable skill: write a draft first, then make it precise, and finally see whether what AI gives can catch your next step.

### The Five "Verb Methods" for Questioning
When writing a question, start with these five verbs and the question becomes specific instantly:
- **Explain**: Please explain… (for understanding)
- **Hint**: Please hint at… (for clues)
- **Compare**: Please compare A and B… (for distinction)
- **Give an example**: Please give a real-life example… (for intuition)
- **Verify**: Please help me verify… (for validation)
Avoid using only one-shot verbs like "write / do / give."

### One Sentence for Parents/Teachers
Encourage children to save their "good questions" into a document, building their own question bank. The class can vote on "best question of the week" so that "knowing how to ask" is seen and rewarded just like "knowing how to answer."
