# 01 · Intro to the AI Study Assistant → My AI Study Rules

## Learning Goals

1. Understand what an "AI study assistant" is, what it can do, and what it cannot do (recognize the limits of large language models).
2. Build the basic mental model that "AI is a sparring coach, not a ghostwriting machine."
3. Personally draft your own "AI Use Code of Conduct" as the baseline for all your future learning activities.
4. Begin to recognize that AI can "confidently say nonsense" (hallucination), and build the habit of questioning it.

---

## Warm-up Question

> One day you come across a math problem you can't solve. Which sentence would you say to AI?
> A. "What's the answer to this problem?"
> B. "Please guide me to understand how to think about this problem. Don't give me the final answer directly — I want to work it out myself."
>
> With these two phrasings, what AI gives you will be completely different. In this session, we start with "figuring out exactly what AI is."

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. What Is an AI Study Assistant
An AI study assistant (such as various conversational large language models) is software that "has read a lot of books, can chat, and can generate text." It is not a person, has no real-life experience, and doesn't truly "understand" the world — it is predicting "the next most reasonable word."

### 2. What It Can Do (for Learning)
- Explain concepts (in words you can understand)
- Give you hints, scaffolding, and ways of thinking — not direct answers
- Grade your homework and give feedback
- Practice with you (speaking, memorizing vocabulary, explaining problems)
- Help you organize, summarize, and ask questions

### 3. What It Can't Do / Where It Goes Wrong
- **Hallucination**: Making up false facts, fake references, and fake data that look very real.
- **It doesn't really think**: It follows your question along; it won't proactively point out that you "asked in the wrong direction."
- **No real-time verification**: It doesn't know today's news and may use outdated knowledge.
- **It doesn't know your exam scope**: It doesn't know how far your teacher has gotten this week.

### 4. The Key Mental Model
> **AI is a sparring coach, not a ghostwriting machine.**
> A coach gives you methods, feedback, and encouragement; but you are always the one on the field playing the game.

## Learning Scenario Example

### Example: Not understanding the physics concept of "buoyancy"
- **Wrong use**: Ask "What is buoyancy? Give me a definition to memorize." → You memorized it, but on the exam, a slightly different question format and you're stuck.
- **Right use**: Ask "Using the bubbles in the straw when I drink soda as an example, can you help me understand why buoyancy points upward? Please guide me, don't give the definition directly." → AI uses a life example you know well and, step by step, builds your intuition.

### Example: Too much history material you can't understand
- **Wrong use**: Throw a passage at AI and say "Summarize it into 200 words." → You didn't read it; you just submitted what it gave.
- **Right use**: "What core event is this passage about? First just list 3 keywords, then I'll read the original myself and check." → You still have to read the original; AI is just navigation.

---

## Correct vs. Incorrect Usage

| Dimension | ❌ Incorrect use | ✅ Correct use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Want the answer, want a finished product | Want thinking, feedback, understanding |
| Questioning | "Help me write an essay" | "Give me 3 opening ideas, then I'll write it myself" |
| After getting results | Copy and submit directly | Retell in your own words, verify, cite sources |
| When confused | Skip it, ask the next thing | Follow up with "why" / "how did this step come about" |
| Attitude | Everything AI says is right | For any factual info, verify before believing |

## Hands-on Task

1. Open your usual AI tool and enter these two sentences separately; observe how the answers differ:
   - "Solve 2x+3=11, give the answer directly."
   - "Solve 2x+3=11, please guide me to understand each step, don't give the final answer directly."
2. Record: In the second answer, which step made you go "Ah, so that's how it works"? Write it down.
3. Find a passage from an AI answer and try to pick out one place that might be wrong (e.g., a specific year, a person's name), then verify it with a textbook or search engine.

## Prompt Template (for middle/high school students)

```
You are a patient middle-school study coach.
Please guide me to understand [subject/topic: ______]; do not give the final answer directly.
I'd like you to:
1. First use examples from my daily life to help me build intuition;
2. Give me hints so I can work it out step by step myself;
3. When I get stuck, give me a bit more of a clue rather than the result directly;
4. Finally, test me with one sentence to check whether I truly understand.
```

> Red-line reminder: Every template carries by default the sentence "Please guide me to understand; do not give the final answer directly."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Treating AI as an encyclopedia**: Assuming everything it says is right and not verifying.
2. **Jumping straight to ghostwriting**: Letting AI write the whole thing while you don't participate — that's the same as not learning.
3. **Not disclosing AI use**: Pasting AI content straight into homework counts as concealment.
4. **Over-reliance**: Asking AI at the slightest difficulty and giving up on your own thinking.

## Homework (Deliverables)

1. Write your own "AI Use Code of Conduct" (at least 5 rules, e.g., "Don't submit AI's original text as homework," "Verify all facts first").
2. Use the "guided template" to ask AI about a concept you recently didn't understand; write 3 sentences each for AI's guidance and your own understanding.
3. Share your code of conduct with a parent/teacher and ask them for one additional suggestion.

## Section Summary & Extension

The AI study assistant is a "sparring coach," not a "ghostwriting machine." Remember three sentences: **It makes mistakes, so verify; it gives answers, so keep asking; it is not responsible, so the responsibility is mine.** The "AI Use Code of Conduct" you created in this session is the foundation of the whole bootcamp — every method in later sessions is built on this code.

### One Sentence for Parents/Teachers
Don't just tell children "Don't use AI to copy homework" — go further and set rules with them for "how to use it correctly." Posting the child's code on the desk works better than a simple ban. When a child can say "AI might be wrong here," they have truly built literacy.

### Extension: The Three-Question Self-Check
Before using any AI content in your homework, ask yourself these three questions:
1. Can I explain this sentence myself? (If not, you didn't understand.)
2. Have I verified this fact? (If not, you can't use it.)
3. Did I write this, or did AI? (If you can't tell, you've crossed the line.)
Only use it once all three pass.
