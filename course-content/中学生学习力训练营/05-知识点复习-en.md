# 05 · Reviewing Knowledge Points → Review Plan

## Learning Goals

1. Use AI to turn "scattered notes" into a "structured knowledge web," improving review efficiency.
2. Make an actionable weekly/monthly review plan, avoiding "cram before the exam, forget after."
3. Let AI generate "quiz questions" to self-test you, instead of memorizing for you.
4. Combine awareness of the forgetting curve to build a "spaced review" habit.

---

## Warm-up Question

> A week before finals, you face 6 subjects of notes and huge pressure. What do you usually do?
> A. Flip through from start to finish, read once, feel "roughly get it."
> B. Have AI help me turn each chapter into "3 core exam points + 1 knowledge map," then have it make 5 self-test questions to quiz me.
>
> B turns "reading" into "testing," with much higher memory retention.

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. The Essence of Review Is "Retrieval," Not "Re-reading"
Psychology research (e.g., the testing effect) shows: active recall (self-testing) works better for memory than repeated reading. AI excels at "making questions to quiz you."

### 2. Structuring: From Notes to Knowledge Web
Scattered underlined notes are hard to recall. Have AI help you organize a chapter into:
- Core concepts (what is it)
- Key relations (how they connect)
- Easy-mistake points (where you often err)
- Typical problems (how it's tested)

### 3. Spaced Review
Don't memorize everything in one day. Spread content across multiple days; each time, self-test the old content first, then learn new content.

### 4. AI's Correct Role
- Organizer: structure your notes (you provide the material)
- Question maker: make quizzes based on your study scope
- Sparring partner: gives feedback after you answer
- Not: memorize for you, or predict the exam for you

---

## Learning Scenario Example

### Biology: photosynthesis
- **Wrong use**: "Summarize photosynthesis for me to memorize." → You didn't participate; slow to memorize, fast to forget.
- **Right use**: Paste your class notes and ask "Organize this chapter into 'core concepts + easy-mistake points + 3 self-test questions'; let me answer the questions myself, then give me feedback."

### History: the 1911 Revolution
- **Wrong use**: Have AI write a review outline to memorize directly.
- **Right use**: "I organized the 1911 Revolution on a timeline. Please help me check which 3 key nodes I missed, and make 2 cause–effect questions of 'reason — impact' to quiz me."

---

## Correct vs. Incorrect Usage

| | ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review method | Re-read notes | Self-test + structuring |
| AI role | Memorize for you | Help organize, make questions |
| Plan | None, cram before exam | Weekly/monthly spaced plan |
| Effect | Short-term, easily forgotten | Long-term retention |

---

## Hands-on Task

1. Pick a unit you recently studied, paste your notes/textbook key points to AI.
2. Have it organize into "core concepts + easy-mistake points + knowledge map (text version)."
3. Then have it make 5 self-test questions (covering different angles); you answer closed-book.
4. Record the wrong questions in the "key review" column of your "review plan."

---

## Prompt Template (for middle/high school students)

```
I am a [grade ______] student, reviewing [subject/unit: ______].
Below are my note key points: [paste ______].
Please do not memorize for me. Please:
1. Organize into three parts "core concepts + key relations + easy-mistake points";
2. Draw a text-version knowledge map (using indentation/arrows to show relations);
3. Make [5] self-test questions to quiz me, covering different angles;
4. I'll answer myself first, then send to you, and you give feedback.
```

> Red line: AI quizzes you, doesn't memorize for you; what's finally remembered must be you yourself.

---

## Human Review Checklist (student self-check)

- [ ] I provided my own notes as material; AI only organized.
- [ ] I did the self-test questions, answering before seeing feedback.
- [ ] I made a spaced review plan (with review times).
- [ ] The points I got wrong were recorded in "key review."

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Letting AI memorize for you**: memorizing the outline directly without your own retrieval.
2. **Only organizing, not testing**: the knowledge map looks nice but isn't really memorized.
3. **No-plan cramming**: no review normally, panic only before exams.
4. **Not reviewing wrong points**: stepping into the same pit next time.

## Homework (Deliverables)

1. Make a "weekly review plan table": list 3 subjects, 20 minutes of daily self-test content, weekend review.
2. Use the template to genuinely generate "knowledge map + 5 self-test questions" for 1 unit, answer them yourself, and record mistakes.
3. One week later, re-test with the same self-test questions and compare accuracy change.

## Section Summary & Extension

The essence of review is "active retrieval," not "repeated reading." Having AI structure your notes and then quiz you sticks far better than dry reading. Spreading review across 20 minutes a day beats an all-nighter before the exam — that's the power of "spaced review."

### Did You Know: The Forgetting Curve
Psychologist Ebbinghaus found that without review, you forget about two-thirds of what you learned after 1 day; but if you review once on day 1, day 3, day 7, and day 30, memory stays long-term. Schedule your review plan to this rhythm: same day, next day, weekend, month-end.

### One Sentence for Parents/Teachers
Help children change "review" from "reading notes" to "doing self-tests." You can spend 5 minutes a week quizzing them with their own self-test questions — being able to answer means truly knowing. Use self-test accuracy as a progress gauge; it reflects real mastery better than scores.

### Quick Self-Check Card (30 seconds opening the manual)
- Did I do "active retrieval" self-testing for this unit? Yes → continue.
- Did I draw the knowledge map? Yes → continue.
- Did I review the wrong points? Yes → at ease.
Only "reading" without "testing" equals no review — the most overlooked pitfall.
- Tip: Don't over-pack your review plan; leave about 20% blank for sudden tasks, or one interruption can make you abandon it entirely. A plan you can stick to is a good plan. When reviewing on the weekend, prioritize the unit with the "highest wrong rate" — spend effort where it counts most, best efficiency.
