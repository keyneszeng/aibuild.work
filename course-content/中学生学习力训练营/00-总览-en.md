# Middle & High School "AI Learning Power Bootcamp" · Course Overview

> A hands-on bootcamp for middle and high school students built on the principle of "using AI to assist learning, not letting AI replace learning."
> The goal is not to teach you more prompts, but to help you build a set of **transferable learning methods**: knowing how to ask, how to break problems down, how to verify, how to express yourself, and how to hold the line on academic integrity.

---

## I. Course Positioning

This is a dual-track course on "AI literacy + learning methods." AI is the tool; learning power is the goal. We will not teach you how to use AI to copy homework, write essays, or do projects. Instead, we teach you how to make AI your "sparring coach" — it gives you scaffolding, feedback, and follow-up questions, but you are the one who achieves understanding and produces the work.

This course adapts the core ideas of the middle-school AI literacy workshop "Beyond AI Hype" (Chinasa T. Okolo, RESPECT'24), the Prompt engineering guides (dair-ai / Learning Prompt), and the introductory AI course "Microsoft AI-For-Beginners," redesigned around the real learning scenarios of Chinese middle and high school students (math mistakes, English essays, history materials, physics concepts, etc.).

---

## II. Target Audience

- **Middle school students (grades 7–9)**: Just starting to use AI tools; they need to build correct usage habits and a sense of boundaries.
- **High school students (grades 10–12)**: Facing more complex academic tasks and college-prep pressure; they need to use AI to boost efficiency without becoming dependent or plagiarizing.
- **Teachers / parents / tutors**: Who wish to guide students to use AI responsibly, rather than banning it outright.

---

## III. Students' Real Pain Points

1. **Asking for the answer right away**: Treating AI as an upgraded search engine — copy the answer and forget it; still can't solve it on the exam.
2. **Not knowing how to ask**: Describing the problem vaguely ("help me write an essay") yields something useless.
3. **Giving up at the first difficulty**: Asking AI for the final result right away, skipping the most valuable "thinking process."
4. **Unable to tell true from false**: AI can confidently say nonsense (hallucination), and students copy it straight into homework and exams.
5. **Academic integrity risk**: Copying AI-generated passages directly, not realizing this counts as plagiarism.
6. **Not reviewing**: Doing many problems but keeping no mistake notebook or review plan; knowledge stays scattered.

Every session of this bootcamp revolves around the idea: "**Let AI assist, but let the learning happen inside you.**"

## VI. Usage Guide for Three Scenarios

### Scenario A: School Classroom (teacher-led)
- Each class is 45 minutes: 10 min explanation + 20 min hands-on task + 15 min showcase and peer review.
- Teachers use the template library (09) for consistent wording, so students don't each write their own and end up with uneven quality.
- Sessions 06 / 10 are recommended to be integrated into "information literacy" or "homeroom/school-based classes," linking with the school's integrity education.
- Equipment: School computer lab or students' own devices, with internet-connected AI tools (e.g., an education edition purchased by the school).

### Scenario B: Training Institution / Club (mentor-led)
- Can be compressed into 4–8 workshops, 90 minutes each, combining two sessions per meeting.
- Emphasis on a "portfolio": at the end, students submit a complete learning-power portfolio (the deliverables above, packaged together).
- Use the grading rubric (at the end of each session) for process-based assessment, not just final results.

### Scenario C: Self-Study at Home (student + parent)
- Students self-study in order 01→08, completing each session's "Homework (Deliverables)" independently; parents do a light check using the "Grading Rubric."
- Parents should focus on 10 (academic integrity) and 06 (true/false info) to help children hold the red lines.
- Recommended pace: 1–2 sessions per week, finishing in 8 weeks; the template library (09) can serve as a quick-reference cheat sheet.

---

## VII. Red Lines Throughout the Course

1. **Don't encourage copying answers directly**: For every AI reply, require the student to "explain it back in their own words."
2. **Every example must require the student to explain their understanding**: Not understanding = not learned; even if AI gives the answer, the student must be able to explain it clearly.
3. **Fact-checking + academic integrity are mandatory**: 06 and 10 are hard requirements, and the other sessions also embed verification steps.
