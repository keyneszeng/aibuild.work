# Children's "AI Creativity Camp" · Course Overview

> This is an "AI creativity" course for kids aged 6–12 to take together with mom or dad.
> We don't teach complicated technology. We do just three things: **get to know AI, learn how to ask good questions, and use AI to turn the ideas in your head into stories and pictures.**
> Remember one sentence: AI is a smart helper that exercises our brains, not a teacher who knows everything.

---

## 1. What This Course Is About

This is a beginner course in "AI literacy + creativity." We treat AI like a box of crayons, a storybook, and a chatty little friend — it helps us draw faster and think further, but **the real imagination and the final decisions always belong to the child.**

This course takes the good methods from three trusted sources and turns them into something young children can understand and do with their hands:

- **Middle-school AI literacy workshop (Beyond AI Hype, Chinasa T. Okolo, RESPECT'24):** teaches kids to use AI critically, to spot AI-generated content, and to understand that AI can also make mistakes.
- **Prompt Engineering Guide (dair-ai / Prompt Engineering Guide):** teaches kids "how to say things clearly so AI can understand."
- **AI For Beginners course (Microsoft AI-For-Beginners):** explains "what AI really is" in the most everyday way.

What we did is turn those big-kid methods into games that 6–12 year-olds can play and do.

---

## 2. Who It's For

- **Ages 6–8 (Little Ones group):** need more company from mom and dad; the focus is on "playing" and "feeling" — drawing a picture or making up one sentence of a story is enough.
- **Ages 9–12 (Little Experts group):** can operate on their own; the focus is on "thinking" and "doing" — finish a fairy tale, a character card, and a little picture book.
- **Parents:** you are the child's most important "safety gatekeeper" and "cheerleader" — just keep them company, ask questions, and give praise.

---

## 3. Four Core Goals

1. **AI is a tool, not an authority:** AI can help, and it can also make mistakes. You must judge the answers yourself and never believe blindly.
2. **Say what you want clearly:** learn to express yourself clearly, like "I want AI to help me…," so AI can actually help.
3. **Turn ideas into creations:** use AI together to write stories, draw characters, and design inventions — produce work that belongs to the child.
4. **Build three safety habits:** protect privacy, tell real from fake, and don't let AI think for you.

## 6. Three Sentences for Parents to Accompany

1. **Ask more, do less for them:** when the child is stuck, first ask "what do you want it to become?" instead of typing for them.
2. **Praise the process more:** praise "what a great idea you had" or "you asked so clearly," not just "it looks so real."
3. **Hold the safety line:** don't enter private info, don't believe everything AI says, and you decide the screen time.

---

## 7. Red Lines Running Through the Whole Course

1. **AI is not the standard answer:** for anything AI gives, make the child think "is this right? does it match what I know?"
2. **Creating comes first, tests come later:** we make stories, drawings, and inventions — no quizzes, no rankings.
3. **Don't leak privacy:** names, schools, addresses, phone numbers, photos — never type them into AI.
4. **Don't skip your own thinking:** AI is a partner that "thinks with you," not a machine that "thinks for you."

---

## 8. General Safety Rules (Kids Version)

- 🔒 **Don't tell AI your secrets:** your real name, school, home address, and phone number — never type them in.
- 🤔 **AI can be wrong:** if what AI says is different from your books or what your teacher said, ask mom or dad first.
- 🎨 **The ideas must come from you:** let AI help you draw and write, but the "ideas" for the story and invention must come from your own brain.
- ⏰ **Agree on screen time:** make a deal with mom and dad about how long each day, and when time's up, turn it off and go play for real.
