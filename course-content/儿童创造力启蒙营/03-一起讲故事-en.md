# 03 · Story Time → Original Fairy Tale

## Learning Goals

1. Experience "AI as a writing buddy": the child supplies the idea, the main character, the opening — AI helps make the sentences sound nicer and continues the story.
2. Understand one key rule: **the story's idea must come from your own head.** AI is a "little secretary" that helps you write the idea beautifully and continue it — not the author of the story.
3. Practice turning a fuzzy imagination into a clear "three story elements": main character, problem, and a wonderful ending.
4. Produce an original little fairy tale designed by the child, and be willing to read it to the family.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Have you ever had this moment: a super idea pops into your head — "wouldn't it be great if a little rabbit could drive a train!" — but when it comes to actually writing a story, you don't know where to start.
>
> Today, AI becomes your "story secretary." You tell it: who's the main character, what trouble they meet, and how you want it to end. It helps make the sentences more fun and writes a few lines to continue. **But remember: that train was your idea first!**

---

## Key Concept: A Good Story Starts with Three Questions

No matter how long the story, it can come from three questions:

1. **Who is the main character?** A little cat afraid of the dark? A little elf who's always late?
2. **What trouble do they meet?** The stars disappeared? Their paintbrush was blown away by the wind?
3. **What happens then? (your wonderful idea)** Does it ask the moon for help? Does it figure out a way itself?

Get these three questions clear, and the story has its bones; AI helps grow the flesh and the pretty sentences.

---

## Activity: Write an Original Fairy Tale

Prep: paper and pencil, or ask a parent to type into an AI tool.

1. **Think of a character (1 minute):** draw or write down a main character on paper, like "a chubby penguin who wants to learn to dance."
2. **Think of the trouble:** what hard thing does it meet? Like "it's too heavy, always falls, and the other penguins laugh at it."
3. **Think of your ending:** how do you want it to wrap up? Like "it discovers that being chubby can roll the cutest snowball dance."
4. **Ask AI for help (clear version):**
   > "I want AI to help me write a 5-sentence little fairy tale. The main character is a chubby penguin who wants to learn to dance; the trouble is it's too heavy and always falls and gets laughed at; the ending is it rolls the cutest snowball dance. Please make it more vivid, but keep all the ideas as I thought."
5. **Edit it:** after AI writes, circle your favorite sentence, and also change one thing you're not happy with (like swap a name).
6. **Read it out:** read the final version to mom and dad, louder and with some expressions to make it more fun!

---

## Parent Tip

- The thing we fear most at this step is **doing it for them.** Parents only type and read aloud; the idea, the character, and the ending must come from the child.
- If the child says "I don't know what to write," guide with questions: "what animal do you like most? what is it most afraid of?" — don't give the answer directly.
- Let the child **edit AI's draft by hand** — even changing just one word is exercising the sovereignty of "I am the author."

---

## Safety Reminder

- In the fairy tale, **don't write in real names, schools, or home addresses.** Using an animal or a fake name is safest.
- Tell the child: the story AI writes is "made up," not real history or news — don't present it as fact to other kids as if it were true.
- If AI writes something weird, scary, or uncomfortable, turn it off right away, tell a parent "I don't like this part," and we'll change the wording together.

## Wrapping Up

Today you were a real "little author": the character, the trouble, the ending — all thought up by you, AI only helped make the story prettier and flow better. **The idea is always your own** — that is the most important rule of creating with AI.
