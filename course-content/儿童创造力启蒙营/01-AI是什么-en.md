# 01 · What is AI → Draw My AI Helper

## Learning Goals

1. Understand "what AI is" in words a child can grasp: it's like a very obedient, very helpful smart little friend — but it's not a real person, and not a teacher who knows everything.
2. Understand that AI is a **tool**: it helps us draw, think up stories, and answer questions, but we are the ones who make decisions and come up with ideas.
3. Use your imagination to draw what "my AI helper" looks like, and give it a name.
4. Build the first safety awareness: AI is amazing, but it can't see you or touch you, so **don't tell it your secrets**.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Little friend, have you ever wondered: that "thing" in your phone that answers as soon as you ask and draws as soon as you speak — what on earth is it?
>
> It lives inside the screen, invisible and untouchable. You tell it to draw and it draws a cat wearing a hat; you tell it to tell a story and it tells one about a flying little pig. Is it a person? No. Is it magic? No either.
>
> It is a "smart tool" called **AI (Artificial Intelligence)**. Today we're going to meet it, and draw an AI friend that belongs to you!

---

## Key Concept (in plain words)

- **What is AI like?** Like a super helpful little assistant. You give it clear words and it works hard to help you.
- **What is AI NOT?** Not a real person (it has no body and won't really play with you), not a teacher (it makes mistakes), not an encyclopedia (there are things it doesn't know either).
- **How does AI "think"?** You don't need to understand the mechanism — just remember: the clearer you speak, the more accurately it helps; and for whatever you ask it to do, the idea still comes from you.

---

## Activity: Draw My AI Helper

Prep: a blank sheet of paper, crayons (or draw on a tablet).

1. **Imagine:** what do you want your AI friend to look like? A round little robot? A flying little cloud? Or a little fox wearing glasses?
2. **Draw:** draw it in the middle of the paper. You can add your favorite colors, expressions, and little props (like a paintbrush or a book).
3. **Name it:** give your AI helper a cute name and write it next to its little head. For example, "Xiao Zhi," "Cloudy," or "Helper Fox."
4. **Write one of its powers:** write one sentence under the drawing, like "it helps me draw dinosaurs" or "it keeps me company making up stories."
5. **(Little Experts bonus):** tell mom and dad when you'd want it to help, and when it shouldn't be used.

> Tip: a drawing with "no right answer" is the best drawing. It's fine if it's a little crooked — this is your one-and-only AI friend!

---

## Parent Tip

- The point of this step is to **spark interest** — don't correct the child with "AI isn't like that." Childlike imagination is the seed of creativity.
- If the child asks "how does AI actually work," answer with a metaphor: "it's like a super-fast obedient machine that has read tons and tons of material, so it can guess what you want it to do." No need to go deep into the technology.
- Guide the child to tell "tool" from "friend": you can say "it's like a specially smart crayon, but the one holding the crayon and drawing is you."

---

## Safety Reminder

- Tell the child: AI lives in the screen, and **it never needs to know your real name, school, home address, or phone number**.
- Today we only "draw" AI, not make it do things yet. When we actually use it later, this rule must always hold.
- If the child draws an AI that "remembers all my secrets," take the chance to say: "only real-life good friends get to know secrets; the little friend in the screen only knows the ideas we choose to share."

## Your Creation

- 📝 **A one-sentence intro:** the child can tell parents clearly "what my AI helper is called and what it can help me with."

---

## Wrapping Up

AI is a smart **tool** — not a real person, and not a teacher who knows everything. Today we "met" it for the first time, and drew the AI friend that belongs to you. Remember: when you ask it for help later, **you provide the ideas, and don't share your secrets**. Next time, we'll learn the most important thing — how to say things clearly so AI can understand!
