# 04 · Draw a Character → Character Design + Prompt

## Learning Goals

1. Learn to make a "design card" for a character: name, look, personality, hobby — the more specific you think, the more the drawing matches what's in your heart.
2. Meet a new kids'-version word: **prompt** (that's "the bit of text you tell AI what to draw"). It's like a note to a painter.
3. Turn the character design into a clear "message to AI" so AI can help draw the character for you.
4. Strengthen safety habits: use imagined characters, don't share secrets; AI's drawing may not be right, and you can change it.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Last lesson we wrote the chubby penguin's story. But is that penguin in your head wearing a red scarf or a blue hat? Smiling or cool?
>
> If you only say "draw a penguin," AI might draw a hundred versions and none is what you want. Today we'll do two things: first make an "ID card" for the character (a design card), then translate that ID card into "words for AI" it can understand — and it can draw the one in your heart!

---

## Key Concept: The Character Design Four-Piece Set

Fill in these four for a character and it comes alive:

1. **Name:** Shanshan / Duoduo / Little Iron Hand…
2. **Look:** what color, what body shape, wearing a hat or not, any special marks.
3. **Personality:** brave? sleepy? a bit timid but very kind?
4. **Hobby / signature move:** loves collecting shells, hops when walking, always hugging a book.

> Mini-dictionary: **prompt** = you connect the above into one clear sentence and show it to AI. Like "I want AI to help me draw…"

---

## Activity: Make a Character Card + Write "Words for AI"

Prep: one character design card paper (draw four boxes or four rows).

1. **Fill the design card:** fill your character using the "four-piece set" above. Example:
   - Name: Shanshan (a little hedgehog)
   - Look: orange-yellow, seven star spots on its back, wearing a little blue hat
   - Personality: shy but brave
   - Hobby: counting stars at night, likes to draw
2. **Write the prompt (words for AI):** translate the design card into one paragraph:
   > "I want AI to help me draw a little hedgehog named Shanshan: orange-yellow, seven star spots on its back, wearing a little blue hat, shy but brave, whose hobbies are counting stars and drawing at night. Picture-book warm style, background a little hillside under a starry sky."
3. **Ask AI to draw once:** type this paragraph in and see if the drawing matches your card.
4. **Compare + fix:** what doesn't match? The hat color? The background? Say it more precisely and draw again.
5. **(Little Experts group):** write a "self-introduction" sentence for the character and stick it next to the design.

---

## Parent Tip

- "Prompt" is a new word for the child — explain it as "the note / words for AI," not as a technical concept.
- It's normal for the drawing to "not look right" — this is exactly how to teach the child **iteration**: change one word, look again, slowly get closer to the heart's wish.
- Encourage the child to keep the design card — it's their original "IP," usable later for stories and picture books.

---

## Safety Reminder

- In the character design, **don't put the child's real name, school, or home address.** Use animal names, fake names, imagined names.
- For AI-generated portraits, don't upload photos with the child's real face to "transform" — protect portrait privacy.
- Remind: AI's drawing sometimes has an extra hand or an extra eye — that's normal, it can draw wrong too, which leads nicely into lesson 06 "AI makes mistakes too."

## Wrapping Up

Today we learned a super useful skill: **first think the character specifically (design card), then turn it into words AI can understand (prompt).** Did you notice? The more detailed you say, the more it looks like what you want. Remember: the character is created by you, AI just draws it according to your note.
