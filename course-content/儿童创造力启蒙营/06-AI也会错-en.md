# 06 · AI Makes Mistakes Too → True/False Judgment Game

## Learning Goals

1. Understand in child-friendly words: **AI is not all-knowing; sometimes it "makes up stories" and says wrong things.** This is called "AI can make mistakes too."
2. Through a little game, practice being a "little detective": when you hear what AI says, first think "does this sound right?"
3. Build a three-step judgment method: pause → think → check (ask an adult / flip a book).
4. Strengthen the course-wide red line: the answer AI gives must not be blindly trusted as the standard answer.

---

## Warm-up Question

> You thought AI knows everything? Wrong!
>
> Sometimes AI tells you something **that doesn't exist at all** very seriously and very confidently. For example it says "penguins live in the tropical rainforest," or "the sun comes out to work at night." It sounds perfectly serious, but it's all wrong.
>
> Today we'll play a "mistake-finding detective" game: I'll say a few sentences and you decide which one is a fake made up by AI!

---

## Key Concept: Why Can AI Be Wrong Too?

In plain words for the child:

- AI learned from "having read tons and tons of material," but there are mistakes in that material too, so it can learn wrong.
- Sometimes, to "keep the sentence going," it will **make up an answer that sounds reasonable** — but there's no such thing at all.
- So: **AI speaking confidently ≠ what AI says is definitely right.** Like a little detective, we doubt first, then verify.

> Little rhyme: **when listening to AI, pause 3 seconds first, and ask yourself "is this reliable?"**

---

## Activity: True/False Judgment Game

Prep: parent reads "sentence cards," child holds up ✅ (might be true) or ❌ (definitely made up).

**Round 1 · parent reads, child judges (examples):**
1. "A fish has bones in its belly, so be careful of the bones." — ✅ (true)
2. "The moon is a big light bulb that glows by itself." — ❌ (false, the moon doesn't glow, it reflects the sun)
3. "Bees use their wings to 'talk' (dance) and tell friends where the flowers are." — ✅ (true, bees really do dance)
4. "A giraffe's neck was originally very short, and it stretched it long itself." — ❌ (false, it's evolution)

**Round 2 · let AI make the questions (key practice):**
> Say to the child: "let's ask AI to make up 3 sentences that 'look true but are actually fake,' and you be the detective to find them."
> Write down what AI says, judge each one with the child, and mark which sentence is made up.

**Round 3 · reverse challenge:**
Let the child make up 1 "fake sentence" to test the parent, and feel how easy it is to "make it real" — and thus understand why AI can fool you.

---

## Parent Tip

- Keep the game atmosphere light like "catching a ghost," not like an exam. Getting it wrong is fine; the point is **building the habit of doubting first.**
- If the child can't tell, guide with the "three-step method": pause (don't believe in a hurry) → think (does it match what I know?) → check (book / adult).
- Take the chance to stress: adults, teachers, and books can also be wrong, but "checking from multiple sides" is the steadiest way.

---

## Safety Reminder

- This lesson points it out directly: **AI can make up fake things,** so any "scary / weird / conflicting with your knowledge" information — ask a parent first, don't panic and don't believe.
- Never try something just because "AI said eating XX would do such-and-such"; for body and health matters, only listen to real people and doctors.
- The fake sentences made up in the game are put down after playing — remind the child "this is practice, not real knowledge."

## Wrapping Up

Today we learned a big secret: **AI can say wrong things, even make up stories.** So no matter how confident it is, we must be little detectives — pause first, then think, then check. Carve "don't believe blindly" into your little head, and you'll use AI better than many grown-ups. Next lesson, we'll use AI as an "invention assistant" to design a magical future object!
