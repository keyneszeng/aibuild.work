# 08 · Showcase → AI Picture Book / Portfolio

## Learning Goals

1. Gather all the works from lessons 01–07 into a **portfolio / AI picture book that belongs to the child,** harvesting the pride of completion.
2. Practice "show and tell": present the child's drawings, stories, characters, and inventions to the family, and clearly state "which part I thought up, and which part AI helped with."
3. Strengthen the core understanding: in this book, **all the ideas and notions come from you,** AI is just the little assistant that helped make them prettier and sound nicer.
4. In a warm "camp-closing little showcase," put a period on the camp, and read the safety awareness once more.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Without noticing, you already have so many works: the AI helper portrait, the question comparison card, a fairy tale, a character card, science question cards, the mistake-finding game card, the future invention sheet…
>
> Are they scattered everywhere now? Today we'll give them a home — a book called **"My AI Creativity Portfolio."** Open it, and it's all your own wonderful ideas!

---

## Key Concept: What Is a "Portfolio"

A portfolio is "a book where I gather the good things I've made, in order."

- It proves one thing: **these ideas were all thought up by me,** AI came to help realize them.
- It's also a gift to your future self: flip through it half a year later and you'll laugh "I had such fun ideas back then."
- The proudest sentence when showing: "the story on this page I made up, AI helped me write it more vividly."

---

## Activity: Make Your Portfolio / AI Picture Book

Prep: a folder / a blank notebook, or a document; glue stick, crayons.

1. **Collect (5 minutes):** find all the works from lessons 01–07 and line them up in order.
2. **Make a cover:** write "My AI Creativity Portfolio · Author: [child's fake name]," and draw a little icon representing yourself.
3. **Bind into a book (one work per page):**
   - Page 1: my AI helper in my heart (portrait + name)
   - Page 2: three-kind question comparison card
   - Page 3: my original fairy tale
   - Page 4: my character card + AI portrait
   - Page 5: science question cards (pick 1–2)
   - Page 6: true/false judgment game card
   - Page 7: future invention design sheet
4. **Write a "creation declaration" (child dictates, parent writes or child writes):**
   > "The ideas in this book were all thought up by me; AI is the good helper that helped me realize them. AI can be wrong too, so I always think about it."
5. **Family showcase (10 minutes):** the child flips through the book and tells the family 2–3 pages, focusing on "which was my idea." Parents applaud + one specific compliment.

---

## Parent Tip

- The showcase step **emphasizes encouragement, goes light on correction.** This is a creativity celebration, not a homework check.
- Help the child clearly say "what I thought / what AI helped with" — this echoes the course-wide red line and also trains expression.
- Photograph the portfolio for safekeeping; when the child grows up and looks back, they'll remember "I created with AI when I was little."

---

## Safety Reminder

- If the portfolio is shared with relatives or posted online, **hide real names, schools, home addresses;** use fake names and imagined characters.
- Don't make the child's clear front-face photo public mixed with the works; protect the portrait.
- At the end of the showcase, read the three safety sentences together with the parent again (no leaking secrets / no blind belief / no dependency), as the camp-closing agreement.
