# 05 · Know the World → Science Question Cards

## Learning Goals

1. Treat AI as a "machine that loves answering why," and encourage the child to ask more and be more curious about the world around them.
2. Practice turning "curiosity" into a clear "science question," not just saying "why."
3. Build an important habit: **what AI says must be checked in books and with real teachers / parents,** because it can be wrong.
4. Produce a stack of "science question cards" that belong to the child, protecting their thirst for knowledge.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Little friend, do you have a pile of "whys":
> Why is the sky blue? Why is an elephant's nose so long? Why are bubbles round?
>
> Grown-ups sometimes can't answer, and sometimes just say something casual and move on. Today, we let AI be the "100,000 Whys" little teacher — but we'll be smart little detectives: **what it says, we must check whether it's right!**

---

## Key Concept: A Good Question Looks Like This

Say the "why" clearly, and AI answers clearly:

- 🔴 Too short: "Why do stars shine?" — AI will answer, but you didn't say how old you are or whether you want it simple or complex.
- 🟢 Clear: "I want AI to tell me, in words an 8-year-old can understand, why stars twinkle."
- 🔵 Add imagination (bonus): "…if you can use a little metaphor, like something from everyday life, even better."

> Remember: after asking, write the answer down, then go flip through a science book and ask a science teacher to see if AI was right.

---

## Activity: Make a Stack of "Science Question Cards"

Prep: a few little cards (or sticky notes), one curiosity written on each.

1. **List curiosities (3):** parent and child each think of 1–2, like "why don't cats get hurt jumping from high places?" "how do clouds form?"
2. **Write the clear version:** rewrite each as "I want AI to explain in kid words…"
3. **Ask AI + record answer:** ask AI to answer, and copy the sentence the child can understand onto the back of the card.
4. **One verification step (key!):** pick one card, and with a parent flip through a picture book / check a children's encyclopedia to see if AI's answer matches the book.
   - Matches → mark ✅ "verified" on the card
   - Doesn't match → mark ❓ "ask an adult again," don't rush to believe.
5. **(Little Experts group):** let the child draw a little "my imagined answer" picture for one of the questions.

---

## Parent Tip

- The focus of this lesson is **protecting curiosity** — don't criticize "that's a silly question." The fact that the child is willing to ask is the starting point of a scientist.
- The verification step is the most precious: it teaches the child "don't believe blindly," which matters more than the answer itself. No book? It's fine to say "we'll check at the library this weekend" — that's also a good example.
- If you don't know the child's question either, say openly "I don't know either, let's ask AI together and then check the book together" — that's the best role model.

---

## Safety Reminder

- When asking, **don't type real names, schools, or home addresses**; only ask for knowledge, not "where my home is."
- AI may give a wrong answer with a straight face (next lesson plays with this specifically). So "verification" is not optional, it's required.
- For questions about body, health, and safety (like "is it okay to take this medicine"), **always ask a real person and a doctor** — don't let AI make the call for you.

---

## Your Creation

- 🗂️ **A stack of science question cards (at least 3):** clear question on the front, AI's answer + verification mark (✅/❓) on the back.
- 🔍 **One verification record:** at least 1 card completed the "flip the book / ask an adult" check.
- 🌟 **Optional:** string the question cards into a little ring and hang it by the desk; add new cards anytime, making a "my why museum."

---

## Wrapping Up

There are endless "whys" in the world, and AI can help us find answers, but it is **not the final judge.** Today we made science question cards, and more importantly learned "check after asking." Know how to doubt and how to verify, and you're a little scientist. Next lesson, we'll play a game specifically — see whether AI really makes mistakes!
