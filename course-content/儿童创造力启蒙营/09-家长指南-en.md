# Lesson 9   Parent Guide (How to Accompany Your Child Using AI)

> This camp is for children aged 6–12 and requires a parent's company. This file is for parents to read, to help you become the "co-pilot" for your child's AI use.

## 1. Why Your Child Needs You Along

AI is a tool, not a babysitter. Children aged 6–12:
- Can't tell "is what AI said real or made up" (see Lesson 6 "AI Makes Mistakes Too").
- Easily treat AI as an "authority answer machine" and lose their own thinking.
- Need someone to help turn "fun" into "gain."

Your role is not "teach technology," but "accompany them to ask, accompany them to think, accompany them to make the idea real."

## 2. Four Moves Every Time You Play Together

1. **Ask before answering:** let the child first say what they want to do, then open AI. Don't type for them right away.
2. **Look at the output together:** after AI gives something, discuss with them "is this real? do you think it's good?"
3. **Hands-on making:** every lesson has a creation task (drawing, writing stories, designing inventions) — accompany them to finish it, not just watch the screen.
4. **Collect the works:** save each work into the "AI creation book" (Lesson 8), building a sense of achievement.

## 3. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: My child copies AI's essay directly and hands it to school — what do I do?**
A: Set the rule clearly — AI is a "brainstorm partner," but the final version must be in their own words. Have them give AI requirements and edit AI's draft, rather than copy it wholesale.

**Q: AI answered wrong and the child believed it — what do I do?**
A: That's exactly the Lesson 6 "true/false judgment" practice. Accompany them to verify: "let's check the book / ask the teacher to see if AI was right."

**Q: My child is addicted to chatting with AI and ignores people?**
A: Set screen time (see below). AI is a supplement, not a replacement for real socializing.

**Q: What tool is safe to use?**
A: Prefer products with "kids mode / parent monitoring"; don't use tools that require real name and phone; don't let the child upload photos to unknown platforms.

## 4. Screen Time and Boundary Suggestions

- Single play session ≤ 30–45 minutes (matching each lesson's length).
- Not within 1 hour before bedtime.
- After use, do a "non-screen" extension: draw out the AI-generated story, or make the invention design by hand.

## 5. Parent Red Lines (follow together with the child)

- Don't tell AI your name, school, or home address.
- Don't treat AI as "exam answers."
- Whether AI says "you're great / you're wrong" is just a program — don't take it to heart.
- When you see uncomfortable content, turn it off immediately and tell a parent.

> The goal of accompanying your child with AI: make them better at thinking, better at expressing, more creative — not more dependent on the screen.
