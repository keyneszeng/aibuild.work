# 07 · My Future Invention → Future Object Design Sheet

## Learning Goals

1. Spark creativity that "solves real problems": first think "what's inconvenient in life," then design a future little invention to solve it.
2. Practice using AI as an "invention assistant": it helps you think of functions, write descriptions, and draw the look — but **the invention's inspiration and the problem to solve come from you.**
3. Turn the idea into a "future object design sheet" with name, look, power, and what problem it solves.
4. Strengthen: AI is a partner that helps you clarify your idea, not a machine that thinks up ideas for you.

---

## Warm-up Question

> Is there something that makes you think "if only I had a thing to help me"?
> Like: the backpack is too heavy to carry, you get wet forgetting an umbrella in the rain, mom can never find her keys…
>
> Today you are a "little inventor"! We'll design a **magical object that only exists in the future,** specially solving one small thing that bothers you most. AI comes as your assistant: helps you think the functions through, write the description clearly, and draw the look. But that "what to solve" good idea — is yours!

---

## Key Concept: A Good Invention Starts with "Trouble"

Every child can invent, just by walking these three steps:

1. **Find the trouble:** what's inconvenient today? (too heavy / too messy / too slow / easy to forget)
2. **Think of the magic tool:** if I had one thing, how could it work to solve it? (fly by itself, get lighter, give reminders)
3. **Draw it:** what does it look like? what's its name? what's its best move?

> Where AI can help: make your magic tool more specific, write a product description for you, draw a concept picture.

---

## Activity: Draw a Future Invention Design Sheet

Prep: one big sheet of paper (draw horizontally, more like a blueprint).

1. **Pick a trouble:** chat with the child, pick the "inconvenience" they feel most. Example: "the backpack gets wet on rainy days."
2. **Think of the magic tool:** design a "sun-rain auto backpack" — puts up a little umbrella cover by itself when it rains, folds away when sunny.
3. **Write the four blocks of the design sheet:**
   - Name: sun-rain auto backpack
   - Look: draw a backpack with a "umbrella flower" on top that opens
   - Best power: automatically opens the umbrella cover when it rains, and can also dry
   - What problem it solves: the backpack and books never get wet again
4. **Ask AI for help (clear version):**
   > "I want AI to help me write 3 sentences introducing this invention, and draw a concept picture: a backpack with an umbrella flower on top that opens automatically, in a future-tech picture-book style."
5. **Put together:** stick the child's hand-drawing + AI's description + AI's picture side by side on the design sheet — that's your "patent drawing"!

---

## Parent Tip

- At this step **protecting originality** matters most: let the child say "what to solve" first, then let AI help refine it — the order can't be reversed.
- If the child says "I don't know what to invent," dig from their daily complaints: "what did you say just now annoys you most?" — that's the golden idea.
- Encourage the child to give the invention a weird cute name; creativity is more precious than "reasonableness."

---

## Safety Reminder

- In the invention sheet, **don't write real names, schools, or home addresses;** sign with a fake name or animal name (like "inventor little panda").
- If AI's drawing uses a "person" image, use an imagined character, don't upload real photos.
- Remind: the "functions" AI writes are imagination, not really buildable; this is a creation exercise, not a product promise.
